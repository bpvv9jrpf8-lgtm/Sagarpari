Krishna Prasad Yadav, chief minister of Nepal's Madhesh Province, resigned on Saturday, Nov. 14. He announced the decision while addressing a provincial assembly session that had been called for a vote of confidence, stepping down before voting began. He was expected to fall short of a majority after two coalition partners, the CPN-UML and the Nepali Communist Party (NCP), withdrew their support.

## How the government lost its majority

According to Himal Press, four UML ministers quit the Nepali Congress-led provincial government on Oct. 11, and the party withdrew its support. Four NCP ministers followed on Nov. 6. Under the constitution, a chief minister must seek a vote of confidence within 30 days after a coalition partner withdraws support.

Media reports say Yadav told the assembly that serving the public and keeping people's trust mattered more to him than holding on to the post without the numbers.

## Province chief accepts resignation

Province Chief Surendra Labh Karna accepted the resignation under Article 169 (1) (a) of the constitution. A statement from his office said the current Council of Ministers will continue to act under Article 169 (3) until a new one is formed.

Nepal News reported that the Province Chief's office has also started the process of forming a new government. Under Article 168 (2), assembly members who can command a majority with the backing of two or more parties have been invited to stake a claim to the chief minister's post by 4 p.m. on Friday.

## Who forms the next government?

The Janata Samajbadi Party Nepal is the largest party in the Madhesh Provincial Assembly. Citing sources, Himal Press reported that JSP Nepal, the UML and the NCP are in talks about forming a new government. No party has yet been confirmed to lead it.

The Kathmandu Post reported that Yadav is the fifth chief minister in the current term to announce a resignation during a provincial assembly meeting. Earlier chief ministers had also left office during confidence-vote proceedings or after failing to secure enough support.
