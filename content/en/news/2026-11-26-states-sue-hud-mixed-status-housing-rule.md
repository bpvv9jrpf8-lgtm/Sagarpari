A coalition of states has gone to court to stop a new U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) rule that would deny federal housing assistance to a family if any household member lacks U.S. citizenship or an eligible immigration status. The rule is scheduled to take effect on **Dec. 14**.

## Who sued, and where

According to California Attorney General Rob Bonta's office, the lawsuit was filed on **Nov. 23** in federal district court in Massachusetts. It is co-led by the attorneys general of California, New York and Massachusetts.

They are joined by the attorneys general of Arizona, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Hawai'i, Illinois, Maine, Maryland, Michigan, Minnesota, New Jersey, New Mexico, Oregon, Rhode Island, Virginia, Vermont, Washington, Wisconsin and the District of Columbia, as well as the governor of Pennsylvania.

## What the rule does

Bonta's office says the rule bars mixed-status families from public housing, Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers and project-based rental assistance. Until now, such families could receive prorated aid that covered only the eligible members.

The release says the rule also adds new paperwork burdens for U.S. citizens and older noncitizens, and requires housing authorities and private landlords to notify the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) if they find an undocumented household member.

## The states' arguments

The states make two main claims:

- Section 214 of the Housing and Community Development Act lets mixed-status families receive prorated aid, gives agencies discretion over checking every member's status, and exempts eligible noncitizens 62 and older. The states say HUD went beyond its authority by overriding that law.
- The rule is "arbitrary and capricious," they argue, because HUD did not properly justify it or consider reasonable alternatives.

## How many families

According to AP, a HUD audit conducted with DHS earlier this year found about **20,000** mixed-status households receiving federally funded housing assistance nationwide. Bonta estimates more than 7,000 such households in California alone. "No child should lose their home over a parent's immigration status," Bonta said.

HUD Secretary Scott Turner said in an emailed statement that the rule ensures only American citizens and certain eligible noncitizens live in HUD-funded housing. He said it would reallocate up to **$218 million** to eligible Americans on waitlists.

## What Nepali families should do

Unless a court blocks it, the rule is set to take effect Dec. 14. Nepali families living in HUD-assisted housing should read any notice from their housing authority or landlord carefully and keep citizenship or immigration documents ready. If anyone in the household has an unclear status, get advice before signing any form. You can also read our [earlier report](/en/news/hud-housing-eligible-status-rule-mixed-families/) on the rule.
