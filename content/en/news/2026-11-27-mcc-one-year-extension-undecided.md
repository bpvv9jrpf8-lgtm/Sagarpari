The projects under the US-funded **Millennium Challenge Corporation (MCC) Nepal Compact** will not be finished on schedule, and Nepal's government has still not acted on a request to extend the deadline by a year, The Kathmandu Post reported Friday. The implementing agency, **MCA-Nepal**, made the request about six months ago.

## What the compact is

The MCC compact is a joint initiative of Nepal and the United States. The US initially committed $500 million and Nepal is contributing $197 million. In November 2025 the US announced an additional $50 million grant, bringing the compact to $747 million.

The five-year agreement was signed in September 2017, ratified by Nepal's House of Representatives in February 2022, and entered into force on Aug. 30, 2023. The projects are due to be completed by **Aug. 30, 2028**.

The compact covers a 315-km, 400 kV transmission line and the upgrade of a section of the Mahendra Highway.

## Why an extension is needed

According to the Post, MCA-Nepal sent a proposal to the Finance Ministry in mid-May asking it to formally request a one-year extension from the MCC. One MCA-Nepal official said that without an extension, neither project would be completed by the August 2028 deadline.

MCA-Nepal points to the Trump administration's 2025 suspension of MCC operations and its July 2025 halt of payments. Earlier, in October 2023, MCA-Nepal cancelled the bids for building the transmission line after every bidder quoted far above the estimated cost, and restarted procurement.

In early January, the US Senate passed legislation allowing the compacts in Nepal, Indonesia, Kosovo and Senegal to be extended by up to one year. Nepal still has to approve the extension on its side.

## Where the decision is stuck

Nepal's Cabinet must endorse the request before it goes to the MCC. The Finance Ministry says it took the matter to the Prime Minister's Office, where there has been "no progress." A senior Finance Ministry official said there "should be no issue with extending the grant project," but no decision has been made.

The US Embassy and senior MCA-Nepal officials have raised the issue with Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle. An MCA-Nepal official told the Post that MCC headquarters "is positive about the proposal because our reasons are well-founded and valid."
