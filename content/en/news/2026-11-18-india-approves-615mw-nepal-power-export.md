India has approved the export of an additional 615.02 megawatts of electricity from Nepal, according to Achut Ghimire, spokesperson for the Nepal Electricity Authority (NEA). The approval follows proposals the NEA had sent to the Indian side earlier, and the export windows start on November 18.

Nepal was already cleared to export 654 MW to India. With the new approval, its total approved export capacity rises to 1,269.02 MW.

## How the power will be sold

The electricity will be sold on the Indian Energy Exchange (IEX) through two Indian power trading companies, PTC India and NVVN (NTPC Vidyut Vyapar Nigam):

- **204.58 MW** from 12 hydropower projects, through PTC, from Nov. 18, 2026 to June 14, 2027.
- **21.34 MW** from the Seti hydropower project, through PTC, from Nov. 18, 2026 to Aug. 31, 2027.
- **389.10 MW** through NVVN via the Dhalkebar–Muzaffarpur line, from Nov. 18, 2026 to June 14, 2027.

Power will cross the border on the 400 kV Muzaffarpur–Dhalkebar double-circuit line and the 132 kV Kataiya–Kushaha, Raxaul–Parwanipur and Ramnagar–Surajpura lines.

## More proposals pending

The NEA said approvals for some other proposals are still being processed on the Indian side. The 12 projects exporting through PTC were not named in the report, and no breakdown was given for the 389.10 MW going through NVVN.

In the last fiscal year, Nepal earned about Rs 18 billion from net electricity exports to India.

## Imports and exports at the same time

Earlier this week, India's Central Electricity Authority also cleared Nepal to import up to 350 MW from India this winter. Under that approval, imports are allowed 17 hours a day, outside the morning and evening peak hours.
