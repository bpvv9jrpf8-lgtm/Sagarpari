Immigrants facing deportation will have much less time to appeal. The Justice Department's Executive Office for Immigration Review (EOIR) published a final rule in the Federal Register on Tuesday, Nov. 17, that cuts the deadline to appeal an immigration judge's decision to the Board of Immigration Appeals (BIA) from 30 days to 10 days.

The rule takes effect 30 days after publication, on Dec. 17.

## What changes

The rule finalizes an interim rule issued in February. The department says it received 1,615 public comments and made only minor changes. The main provisions:

- **10-day appeal deadline:** In most cases, an appeal must be filed within 10 days of the immigration judge's decision. The clock starts on the day the judge states an oral decision.
- **Asylum exception:** Asylum cases keep the 30-day deadline. But if a person is found ineligible under certain legal bars — such as missing the one-year filing deadline, a safe-third-country agreement, or a previous asylum denial — the 10-day deadline applies.
- **Fast dismissals:** A single BIA member may dismiss an appeal without a written opinion within 15 days of filing, unless a majority of the Board's permanent members vote within 10 days to send it to the full Board.
- **Briefs:** In appeals that are not summarily dismissed, both sides file briefs at the same time, within 20 days of the Board setting a schedule. No reply briefs are allowed, and extensions are granted only in exceptional circumstances.
- **All grounds up front:** Issues not raised on the Notice of Appeal (Form EOIR-26) are treated as waived.

## Provisions a court had blocked

In March 2026, a federal district court in Washington, D.C., in Amica Center for Immigrant Rights v. EOIR, set aside parts of the interim rule — the 10-day deadline, the waiver provision and the 15-day dismissals — ruling that the government had skipped required notice and comment. With the comment process now complete, the department says it has fully met the requirements of the Administrative Procedure Act.

## What it means for Nepalis

Nepalis with asylum or other cases in immigration court should contact a lawyer as soon as a decision is issued. For people without a lawyer, preparing a detailed appeal in 10 days may be hard. Check with an attorney which deadline applies to your case, based on the type and date of the decision.
