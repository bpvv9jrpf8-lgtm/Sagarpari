The stopgap bill that keeps the federal government running expires on **Dec. 11, 2026**. If Congress doesn't pass new funding by then, parts of the government could shut down starting Dec. 12. As of Nov. 18 there was no deal, Latin Times reports.

The main sticking point this time is spending on the war with Iran. Funding bills need 60 votes in the Senate, NPR notes, so Republicans need Democratic votes even though they hold the majority.

## What's the fight about?

Some Democrats, including Sen. Tim Kaine of Virginia, want the stopgap bill to include a provision ending funding for the Iran war. Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland backs that approach. Sen. Cory Booker, however, says the party must avoid a shutdown.

On the Republican side, Sen. Thom Tillis says Congress needs to replenish weapons stockpiles such as Patriot missiles. President Trump is pushing a $350 billion defense package through a separate process known as reconciliation, which some lawmakers say raises the risk of a shutdown.

## What it could mean for immigrants and workers

According to Latin Times, a funding lapse after Dec. 11 would:

- Freeze the Labor Department's **FLAG** online filing system, which handles applications such as H-1B labor condition applications (LCAs) and PERM labor certifications for green cards.
- Suspend the legal authority for **E-Verify**, the system employers use to confirm workers are allowed to work.
- Suspend authority for the **EB-4 religious worker** visa, the **Conrad 30** program for doctors and supplemental **H-2B** visas.
- Leave **USCIS** largely running, because it is funded mainly by application fees.

Elsewhere, the IRS would suspend audits and phone lines, while Social Security, Medicare and the Postal Service would keep operating. Federal employees get back pay after a shutdown; government contractors do not have that guarantee.

## It has happened before

The government shut down for 43 days, from Oct. 1 to Nov. 12, 2025. Then, starting Feb. 14, 2026, funding for the Department of Homeland Security lapsed for 76 days.

Readers whose jobs or cases depend on H-1B filings, PERM or E-Verify may want to talk with their employer or attorney early. SagarPari will keep following Congress ahead of Dec. 11.
