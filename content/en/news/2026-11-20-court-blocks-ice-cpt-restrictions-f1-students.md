A federal judge has blocked a government directive that tightened Curricular Practical Training (CPT), the internship and work-training option for international students on F-1 visas. On November 17, Judge Julia E. Kobick of the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts granted a preliminary injunction and a stay of agency action.

## What the directive said

The Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP), part of Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), sent two "broadcast" messages to schools on August 12 and August 24, 2026. According to the court's order, the messages said CPT could be authorized only if it was required for all students in that degree, and that CPT could not be offered as part of an elective course. They warned that schools that did not comply could lose their SEVP certification to enroll foreign students.

That made elective internships taken for academic credit ineligible for CPT. Judge Kobick found this reading departed from the government's position since 1991, without the government explaining the change.

## Who sued

The Association of American Universities (AAU), NAFSA: Association of International Educators, the Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration, and the Association of Independent Colleges and Universities in Massachusetts (AICUM) sued the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), ICE and others.

The order notes that after the directive, Columbia University had to deny fall CPT to more than 120 international students, and at Grinnell College at least 13 students lost internship opportunities.

## What happens now

Under the order, the two August messages may not be enforced or applied while the case continues. The government is also barred from applying the new CPT interpretation to the plaintiff organizations and their members, including their employees and students. The court did not order the government to reverse adverse actions already taken or to send written notice to every SEVP-certified school.

The Harvard Crimson reported that UC Berkeley has resumed processing CPT requests after the ruling. Reuters reported that DHS declined to comment, and ICE told The Crimson it would not comment on pending litigation. It is not yet known whether the government will appeal. Citing ICE data, Reuters reported that 140,775 student visa holders were authorized for CPT in 2024.

Nepali students who want to do an internship through CPT should check with their school's international student office or DSO about whether their school is covered by the order and what its current guidance is.
