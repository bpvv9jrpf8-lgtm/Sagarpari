Foreign investors are still pulling back from Nepal. Figures released Tuesday by Nepal's Department of Industry show foreign direct investment (FDI) commitments of just **Rs 19.55 billion** in the first four months of fiscal year 2026/27, from mid-July to mid-November.

In the same period last fiscal year, commitments stood at **Rs 36.68 billion**. That is a drop of about **46.7%** in one year.

## More projects, less money

The department approved 497 foreign-investment projects during the period. According to HimalPress, there were 382 in the same period last year. So the number of projects went up while the money went down — most of the new projects are small.

The jobs picture follows the same pattern. The approved projects are expected to create 6,201 jobs, compared with 33,814 jobs projected from commitments in the same period last year.

In the most recent month alone (the Nepali month of Kartik), 85 projects brought commitments of Rs 3.42 billion.

## Where the money is going

Information and communication technology (ICT) leads by number of projects, but tourism leads by value:

- **Tourism:** 146 projects, Rs 8.67 billion (the largest amount)
- **Energy:** 3 projects, Rs 4.05 billion
- **Services:** 44 projects, Rs 3.95 billion
- **Manufacturing:** 29 projects, Rs 1.75 billion
- **ICT:** 272 projects, Rs 979.6 million
- **Agriculture and forestry:** 3 projects, Rs 150 million

Mining and infrastructure drew no foreign investment commitments at all during the period.

## Why it matters to Nepalis abroad

Foreign investment plays a big role in creating jobs and growth in Nepal. The first-quarter figures had already shown commitments down by more than half from a year earlier. For Nepalis in the US (NRNs) thinking about investing back home, the numbers are a signal of how investors view Nepal's business climate.

Keep in mind that these are **commitments**, not money that has actually arrived. Nepal Rastra Bank data cited by HimalPress show that in fiscal year 2024/25, commitments of about Rs 64.96 billion translated into actual inflows of only Rs 12.02 billion, or 18.5%.
