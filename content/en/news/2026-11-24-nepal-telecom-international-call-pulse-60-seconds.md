If your family in Nepal calls you on a Nepal Telecom phone or mobile line, the way those calls are billed has changed. Nepal Telecom has raised the pulse for international outgoing calls from 30 seconds to 60 seconds. The change took effect on Mangsir 8, 2083 (Nov. 24, 2026) and covers calls to every country.

## What changed

According to reports in Kantipur and Nagarik, Nepal Telecom announced the change in a public notice. Until now, a 60-second pulse applied to calls to only 58 countries, under a decision dated Falgun 1, 2082. Calls to all other countries were billed in 30-second pulses. Now one 60-second pulse applies everywhere.

Kantipur reports that the new pulse applies to calls made using the international access codes **00, 1424, 1425 and 1445**.

The company says the change follows a Bhadra 24 decision by the Nepal Telecommunications Authority under Section 42 of the Telecommunications Act, 2053. Nagarik cited Nepal Telecom spokesperson Dr. Kamal Lamichhane.

## What is a pulse?

A pulse is the unit used to bill a phone call. With a 30-second pulse, a call was charged in 30-second blocks. With a 60-second pulse, it is charged by the full minute. That means a short call, or a call that ends partway through a minute, can be charged for a full pulse.

Neither report says whether per-minute rates have changed. Both say the rate for each country can be checked on Nepal Telecom's website.

## For readers in the US

The rule applies to calls made from Nepal on Nepal Telecom's service. The reports say nothing about calls from the US to Nepal or about incoming calls. If parents or relatives in Nepal call you regularly, it may help to let them know about the change. They can check the rates on Nepal Telecom's website or ask the company directly.
