Workers who save for retirement through a 401(k) will be able to put away $1,000 more in 2027, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) announced on Thursday, November 12. The limit for individual IRAs, however, is not going up.

## What changed

The amount employees can contribute to 401(k), 403(b), most governmental 457 plans and the federal Thrift Savings Plan (TSP) rises to **$25,500** for 2027, up from $24,500 in 2026, the IRS said.

The catch-up contribution for workers aged 50 and over goes from $8,000 to **$8,500**, so people in that group can contribute up to $34,000 in total. For workers aged 60 through 63, the catch-up limit is **$11,750**.

The annual limit for individual retirement arrangements (IRAs) stays at **$7,500**. The IRA catch-up for people 50 and over also stays at $1,100.

The limit for SIMPLE retirement accounts rises from $17,000 to $17,500.

## Roth IRA and deduction income ranges

The income ranges that phase out Roth IRA contributions move up slightly:

- **Single filers and heads of household:** $158,000 to $173,000 (up from $153,000 to $168,000 in 2026).
- **Married couples filing jointly:** $250,000 to $260,000 (up from $242,000 to $252,000).

For single filers covered by a workplace retirement plan, the phase-out range for deducting traditional IRA contributions is now $83,000 to $93,000. For married couples filing jointly where the spouse making the contribution is covered by a workplace plan, it is $133,000 to $153,000.

## Why it matters

Many Nepalis working in the U.S. — green card holders, citizens and people on work visas — save for retirement through an employer's 401(k), and many employers add matching contributions. The new limits take effect January 1, 2027, so anyone who wants to save more next year can adjust their contribution rate through payroll or HR.

The IRS lays out all of the 2027 adjustments in Notice 2026-66.
