**Human Rights Watch (HRW)** is urging Nepal's government to crack down on illegal moneylenders. In a report released Nov. 25, the group says the conflict in the Gulf has left many migrant workers unable to travel for work or send money home, pushing them and their families deeper into high-interest debt.

## Fewer Gulf jobs, same loans

According to the report, Nepal suspended labor permits for Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar and Kuwait in March 2026. Citing Department of Foreign Employment data, HRW says Gulf-bound labor approvals fell from 228,165 to 109,249 between late February and mid-September, a drop of about **52%**.

Workers who borrowed to pay for migration but could not leave still face lenders demanding repayment, the report says.

## Fake contracts and "meter byaj"

HRW interviewed 18 borrowers, along with local officials, lawyers and activists, in six Terai districts, and examined more than 50 cases in Nawalparasi.

The report describes:

- **Kapali tamasuk**, informal loan contracts that can list a principal three to 10 times the cash the borrower actually received.
- **Meter byaj** loans, with interest of 36% to 60% a year or more, compared with under 10% at banks.
- Land seizures, evictions, threats and beatings when borrowers can't pay, as well as sexual exploitation of women.

Victims say police often dismiss their complaints as private disputes, and that lenders have used blank checks and disputed contracts to threaten borrowers with arrest.

## A law on paper

Nepal made fraudulent informal lending a crime, an "improper transaction," on May 5, 2023, punishable by up to seven years in prison. But more than 65,000 complaints filed with successive inquiry commissions remain unresolved.

The Kathmandu Post reports that the government signed a nine-point agreement with loan-shark victims' representatives on July 17, after an 11-day march from Janakpur to Kathmandu.

"Despite government commitments to protect borrowers, illegal practices are rife in Nepal's informal credit sector," said **Meenakshi Ganguly**, HRW's deputy Asia director.

## What HRW wants

- End arbitrary arrests of borrowers
- Prosecute lenders who use violence and threats
- Stop land transfers and evictions based on illegal loan contracts
- Set up working debt-relief and grievance systems
- International partners should help expand affordable bank credit and stronger protections for migrant workers
