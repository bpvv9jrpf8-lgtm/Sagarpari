At least eight people were killed when a three-story building collapsed in Brooklyn on Wednesday afternoon. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani said Thursday morning that the death toll had reached eight and at least 17 people were injured, including three firefighters.

According to the Associated Press, two deaths were announced Wednesday and the rest were confirmed Thursday. Authorities said late Wednesday that at least seven children were among the dead or injured. It is not clear how many people may still be trapped.

## Where and how it happened

CBS New York reported that the collapse happened just after 4 p.m. on Wednesday, Nov. 11, on Bedford Avenue between Newkirk and Flatbush avenues in Flatbush. The building was a mixed-use structure with apartments above commercial space.

Next door, an empty lot was being excavated for construction. City Buildings Commissioner Ahmed Tigani said Wednesday that workers were excavating the lot when the building buckled, the AP reported. A neighboring building that shared a wall is still standing, with part of its wall torn away.

## Rescue continues

The FDNY said a "major technical rescue operation" was underway. Rescuers searched overnight with dogs. At least one survivor was pulled out, and a pregnant woman was rescued Wednesday night.

FDNY Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore called the work "dangerous and perilous," saying, "Every move is choreographed." She warned that possible rain Thursday afternoon could turn the debris to mud.

"There are no words for a loss of this magnitude," Mamdani wrote on X.

## Work had been stopped before

The city Department of Buildings is investigating the cause. According to the AP, a stop-work order was issued in March after inspectors found the adjacent building was being demolished without adequate overhead protection such as netting. In April, inspectors found demolition had resumed in violation of that order. Work was allowed to continue in May after the problems were corrected. The project has active permits for excavation and foundation work.

Officials have not publicly released the names of the victims.

The FDNY asked people to avoid the area, and the AP reported that Flatbush Avenue was closed to traffic.
