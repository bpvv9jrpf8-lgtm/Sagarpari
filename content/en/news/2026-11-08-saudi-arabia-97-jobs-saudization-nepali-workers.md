**Saudi Arabia**, one of the main destinations for Nepali workers, has tightened rules on hiring foreigners in **97 tourism and administrative jobs**. A rule requiring a set share, or all, of these jobs to go to Saudi nationals ("Saudization") is now in force. According to The Kathmandu Post, about **400,000 Nepalis** currently work in Saudi Arabia.

## 28 jobs in tourism

The first phase of the decision, issued by Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development with the Ministry of Tourism, took effect on April 22, 2026. It applies to private tourism businesses with one or more employees.

- **Saudi nationals only (4 jobs):** receptionist, hotel receptionist, telephone operator and information staff.
- **70 percent Saudi (12 jobs):** including branch manager, hotel inspector, tour guide, purchasing, public relations and sales specialists.
- **50 percent Saudi (12 jobs):** including ticket sales staff, travel agent, event coordinator, marketing specialist, restaurant host, housekeeping supervisor and sales representative.

More is coming. From January 3, 2027, 30 percent of **chef** positions must go to Saudis, and from January 2, 2028, 50 percent of 12 managerial positions. In total, 41 tourism jobs will fall under the rule.

## 69 administrative jobs reserved for Saudis

Sixty-nine administrative and support jobs in the private sector are now fully reserved for Saudi nationals. The ministry said on October 4 that the rule was fully in effect and inspections of companies had begun. The list includes HR and recruitment managers and specialists, administrative assistants, government relations staff, customs representatives, shipping representatives and clerks, stock records clerks and several public relations roles.

The ministry's guidelines say employers who break the rule face legal action.

## How much will it affect Nepalis?

Most Nepalis who go to Saudi Arabia are not in these jobs today. According to Nepal's Labour Migration Report 2024, 67.6 percent of those who received labor permits for Saudi Arabia went into elementary jobs, 12.2 percent worked as drivers and machine operators and 8.7 percent in services and sales. Only 0.8 percent went into office and administrative support work.

But for Nepalis working in hotels, tourism and offices, or planning to, the path has narrowed. Rajendra Bhandari, former president of the Nepal Association of Foreign Employment Agencies, said recruitment of foreign workers into fully Saudized jobs will stop. A Nepali businessperson in Saudi Arabia said companies that miss their Saudization quotas can fall into the red or yellow bands of the Nitaqat system, which can affect workers' visa and iqama renewals.

## Take note

If relatives of yours in Saudi Arabia work in hotels, tourism or offices, encourage them to check with their employer whether their job is on this list. Be wary of manpower agents or middlemen promising new visas for these jobs.
