Nepal's President **Ramchandra Paudel** has not issued two ordinances the government recommended nearly three weeks ago. According to *The Kathmandu Post*, the delay points to a strained relationship between the president and Prime Minister **Balendra Shah**. The government says there is no dispute.

## Which ordinances?

The cabinet recommended two ordinances on **November 3**. Sources at Sheetal Niwas, the president's office, said they arrived there on **November 6**.

- An ordinance amending the **Press Council Nepal Act, 1992**
- An ordinance amending several laws on culture and civil aviation. It changes Section 12 of the **Nepal Airlines Corporation Act, 1962**, to allow temporary appointments when the top post is vacant, and adds an executive chair position.

A third ordinance, to carry out the government's agreement with the Gen Z movement, has also been recommended, so three are now pending. The Post notes that the Shah government has issued 13 ordinances since taking office in March.

## Why the hesitation?

The Post reports that the president believes matters like press freedom and routine executive appointments should go through Parliament's normal lawmaking process. On November 6, a Federation of Nepali Journalists delegation led by its president, **Nirmala Sharma**, met Paudel and urged him not to issue the Press Council ordinance, saying it aims to curtail press freedom. Paudel reportedly said he would consult constitutional experts.

Leadership at Nepal Airlines has been unsettled for months. On August 20, the Supreme Court annulled the appointment of its executive chair, ruling that he was over the 65-year age limit.

## What officials and experts say

Law Minister **Sobita Gautam** denied any rift. She said several public holidays cut into working days and that "the President is likely studying them," but added that the president cannot block recommendations indefinitely.

Senior advocate **Baburam Kunwar**, the president's legal adviser, said the office is still reviewing the drafts and would examine them "within a day or two." Constitutional expert **Chandra Kanta Gyawali** said the government must justify its ordinances, but the president cannot hold them indefinitely.

Under Article 114(1) of the constitution, the president can issue ordinances on the cabinet's recommendation when Parliament is not in session. The constitution sets no deadline for the president to act.

## A widening distance

According to the Post, Shah has rarely briefed Paudel in person. On May 11 he walked out of a joint sitting while the president was reading the government's policies and programs, and he did not meet the president before his November 22–23 visit to India.
