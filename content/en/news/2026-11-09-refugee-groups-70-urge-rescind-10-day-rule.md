Seventy organizations sent a joint letter on Monday to the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) and US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), asking them to rescind a rule that cut the time refugees have to respond in status termination proceedings from 30 days to 10. The letter, led by Global Refuge, the International Refugee Assistance Project (IRAP) and Refugee Council USA, was signed by refugee service, faith, legal aid, veterans and community groups.

## Ten days is too short, groups warn

In the letter to DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin and USCIS Director Joseph Edlow, the groups say a 10-day deadline gives refugees no real chance to defend their status. Refugees with limited English, disabilities or serious health problems, and those in communities with little access to legal help, would be hit hardest, the letter says.

According to DHS's own data, about 186,000 refugees who arrived between fiscal years 2015 and 2025 have not yet received green cards. The letter points out that the rule's own economic analysis admits some refugees who could reaffirm their status in 30 days would not manage it in 10.

## Objection to paused green card processing

The letter raises another serious issue: USCIS has put decisions on more than 155,000 refugee green card applications on hold. With termination risk on one side and stalled green cards on the other, resettled refugees are living in uncertainty, the groups say.

The rule took effect the day it was published, October 14, without a public comment period. A federal district court later temporarily blocked enforcement of the rule and of termination notices already issued under the short deadline. The groups say a court pause is not enough and the rule itself should be withdrawn.

## The letter's four demands

- Immediately rescind the 10-day rule and give refugees a real chance to find a lawyer, see the allegations and submit evidence before any termination is final.
- Fully restart paused green card processing and create a path for seriously ill people to request faster processing through members of Congress.
- End the re-vetting of refugees already in the US.
- Require notice and public comment before any similar rule in the future.

Global Refuge President and CEO Krish O'Mara Vignarajah said no one can meaningfully defend their legal status in 10 days, in a second language, without a lawyer, while gathering documents from the country they were forced to flee.

The issue directly affects the Bhutanese-Nepali refugee community in the US, since many families without green cards and with pending status correspondence fall within this process. If you or your family receive such a notice, contact an immigration attorney or accredited representative right away so you do not miss the deadline.
