Green card holders preparing to become U.S. citizens, including many Nepalis, face an important change. On November 4, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) issued policy memo PM-602-0201, telling officers to look more closely at an applicant's financial conduct when deciding whether they meet the "good moral character" requirement for naturalization.

Under the new guidance, an applicant who willfully fails to support their dependents may be found to lack good moral character. That includes abandoning minor children, giving dependents no support at all, or paying clearly less than a court has ordered. The memo says officers will also consider extenuating circumstances if the applicant can show them.

## Sponsorship duties will be checked too

The memo also covers sponsorship obligations. Failing to meet a support obligation taken on through Form I-864, I-864EZ or I-864A, or failing to keep a financial support commitment made on Form I-134 or I-134A while it is still in effect, may count against the applicant.

The guidance on public benefits is direct as well. Receiving public benefits unlawfully or through fraud, or causing a dependent to receive them that way, may count against an applicant. So may using benefits in a way that shows a willful effort to avoid becoming self-sufficient. But reports on the memo note that lawfully receiving benefits does not, by itself, mean a person lacks good moral character — officers must weigh the facts of each case individually.

## The N-400 form will change

According to the reports, USCIS is also preparing to revise Form N-400, the citizenship application, to ask for more detail about an applicant's financial support obligations and history of public benefits. The agency says it will also update its Policy Manual and other guidance so officers apply the new standard consistently. No date has been announced for when the revised form will take effect.

The good moral character period generally covers the five years before the citizenship application through the oath ceremony. So anyone planning to apply should get their child support, tax and sponsorship records in order ahead of time, and find out early whether any old obligation is still outstanding.

For many Nepalis who have lived in the U.S. for years and are now eligible for citizenship, this change matters directly. If you are unsure whether the new guidance affects your situation, it is wise to get advice before you file.
