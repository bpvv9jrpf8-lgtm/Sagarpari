The approval rate for **EB-1A**, the U.S. green card category for people with "extraordinary ability," has dropped from nearly 75 percent to **32 percent** in two years. The decline shows up in U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) data, according to a Washington Post report published November 27.

## What the numbers show

USCIS I-140 data show that in the third quarter of fiscal year 2026 (April–June 2026), only **32.2 percent** of decided EB-1A petitions were approved — 1,348 approvals against 2,832 denials. That is down steadily over six straight quarters from 74.7 percent in the first quarter of fiscal 2025.

In the first three quarters of fiscal 2026, USCIS approved **5,237** EB-1A petitions and denied **7,639** — an approval rate of 40.7 percent. In the same period of fiscal 2024, it approved nearly 8,900. As of June 30, 2026, **28,874** EB-1A petitions were pending, compared with just 8,088 at the end of June 2023.

## EB-1B and EB-1C hold steady

The drop is limited to EB-1A. Approval rates for EB-1B (outstanding professors and researchers) and EB-1C (multinational executives) remain **above 90 percent**. In other words, the problem is concentrated in the EB-1A self-petition category.

## Why approvals fell

According to immigration attorneys, adjudicators are leaning more heavily on a subjective last-step review known as "final merits" — so petitions that meet three of the 10 legal criteria are still being denied at that stage. USCIS has not explained the decline. The law and regulations have not changed: EB-1A still requires meeting three of 10 criteria, or showing a single major international achievement such as an Olympic medal or an Oscar.

Courts are divided over the final merits process. A federal judge in Nebraska threw it out in January 2026, finding it had been adopted unlawfully, but at least six other rulings have upheld it. The government withdrew its appeal, yet USCIS continues to apply the review.

## What it means for Nepali applicants

Many Nepali professionals, researchers, artists and athletes use the EB-1A route. It is also the category through which First Lady Melania Trump received permanent residence in 2001. In the current climate, strong evidence for every criterion, clear documentation and careful preparation matter more than ever. Lawfully, a firm that analyzes immigration data, projects the rate could fall to about **27 percent** in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026.

If you are preparing an EB-1A petition, organize your evidence around the criteria and talk to an experienced immigration attorney before you file.
