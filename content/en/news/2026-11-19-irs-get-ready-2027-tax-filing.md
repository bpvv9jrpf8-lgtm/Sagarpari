The Internal Revenue Service is urging taxpayers to start getting ready now for the 2027 filing season. In a reminder issued Nov. 16 (IR-2026-140), the agency said it is the first in a series of "Get Ready" notices ahead of filing federal returns in 2027. The advice applies to anyone in the US who works, keeps a bank account or gets paid through online apps — including many Nepali workers and families.

"With start of the filing season just months away, now is the time for taxpayers to prepare to file their 2026 tax return," said IRS Chief Executive Officer Frank J. Bisignano. He advised taxpayers to gather their records, finish year-end transactions on time and learn about tax law changes that affect them.

## Documents to gather

To avoid errors that can delay refunds, the IRS says to collect these before you file:

- Bank routing and account numbers
- **Forms W-2** from every employer, including part-time jobs
- **Forms 1099** from banks and other payers, such as online marketplaces and payment apps
- Records of digital asset transactions

## Paper refund checks are being phased out

The IRS calls direct deposit the fastest way to get a refund. Under Executive Order 14247, the agency began phasing out paper refund checks last year, so most taxpayers will need routing and account numbers.

People without a bank account can look into an FDIC-insured bank or use the NCUA's credit union locator tool. Prepaid debit cards, digital wallets and mobile apps may also accept direct deposit if they provide routing and account numbers tied to a personal account, but the IRS says to confirm with the provider which numbers to use.

## Online account and the IRS app

An IRS Individual Online Account lets you view past tax records and transcripts, make payments or set up a payment plan, get an Identity Protection PIN (IP PIN), give a tax professional digital access, and see available Forms W-2 and certain 1099s. New users verify their identity through Login.gov or ID.me. The IRS app lets you check refund status and find free filing options without signing in.

## New deductions

The IRS and Treasury are continuing work on the new law's provisions for qualified tips, qualified overtime pay, qualified passenger vehicle loan interest and the enhanced deduction for seniors. Updates are posted on the IRS "Working Families Tax Cuts" page — worth watching for anyone who earns tips or overtime in restaurants, hotels or gas stations.

More information is on the IRS [Get Ready to File Your Taxes](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/get-ready-to-file-your-taxes) page.
