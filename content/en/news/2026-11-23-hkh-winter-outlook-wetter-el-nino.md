Nepal and much of the Himalayan region could see more rain and snow than usual this winter, according to the **Hindu Kush Himalaya Winter Outlook 2026**. The outlook was prepared by the International Centre for Integrated Mountain Development (ICIMOD), Beijing Normal University and the Institute of Atmospheric Physics of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, The Himalayan Times reported on Monday.

## What it means for Nepal

The outlook forecasts above-normal precipitation across Nepal, with the strongest signal in central Nepal. The hills may be warmer than normal, while high elevations may be cooler. Snow depth in the north is expected to be above normal.

Above-normal snow depth is also projected for parts of Afghanistan, Pakistan and northern India, while parts of Bhutan and eastern Xizang (Tibet), China, may get less snow than usual.

## Why

The forecast is tied to an expected strong to very strong **El Niño**, the periodic warming of the Pacific Ocean that shifts weather patterns worldwide. According to the report, the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) puts the chance that this El Niño becomes the strongest since records began in 1950 at 60 percent.

## Higher hazard risks

More precipitation also brings risks. The outlook warns of more floods, landslides and avalanches in parts of the western Himalaya. A wetter winter does not rule out forest and grassland fires, because some areas may still have dry spells. Fire risk is rated high to very high in southeastern Xizang and parts of Myanmar.

Winter snow matters well beyond the season: it feeds river flows, drinking water, irrigation, hydropower and mountain springs.

## What experts say

Sarthak Shrestha, the report's lead author, said the outlook gives the eight Hindu Kush Himalaya countries a common picture of the season to coordinate their preparedness. ICIMOD hydrologist Manish Shrestha said disaster preparedness should continue after the monsoon, and that seasonal outlooks should be used alongside short-range weather forecasts.

ICIMOD will present the findings at an international conference on the Hindu Kush Himalaya cryosphere in Kathmandu from Nov. 25 to 27.

**For Nepalis in the US:** If you are planning a winter trip home or a trek in the mountains, check the latest forecasts from Nepal's Department of Hydrology and Meteorology and road-weather updates before you travel.
