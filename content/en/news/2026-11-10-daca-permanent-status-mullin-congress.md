A path to permanent residence for DACA recipients — immigrants brought to the United States as children — is back in the conversation. Homeland Security (DHS) Secretary Markwayne Mullin said on Monday, November 9, that Congress could create such a path.

Speaking to reporters on the sidelines of a counter-drone conference in Maryland, Mullin said that if immigration reform is going to look at DACA and find a way to give those people permanent legal residence, that is a job for Congress. He also said President Donald Trump had told him to start talking with Democrats who have been reaching out about legislation for the new Congress.

But Mullin was just as clear about the limits. He said there will never be amnesty, and that he has not discussed changing the administration's enforcement policy with the president. In other words, the current crackdown on undocumented immigrants stays in place.

Why it matters: according to Fox News, this is the first time Mullin has publicly suggested that DACA recipients could gain permanent status through Congress. DHS itself had previously maintained that DACA confers no legal status — DACA is only a deferral of deportation plus permission to work.

The numbers show how many people this touches. According to USAFacts, drawing on USCIS data, about 455,200 people held active DACA status at the end of March 2026. House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries has said the American Dream and Promise Act could be a starting point — that bill proposed protections for both DACA and TPS holders.

Nothing has changed in anyone's paperwork yet. Granting permanent residence would require Congress to pass a new law, and the 60-vote hurdle in the Senate remains in the new Congress too. So for DACA holders, the most practical step right now is to keep renewing their two-year permits on time — statements from Washington alone don't change anyone's status.
