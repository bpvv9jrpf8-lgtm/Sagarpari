Former Deputy Prime Minister and CPN-UML leader **Top Bahadur Rayamajhi**, who was serving a sentence in Nepal's fake Bhutanese refugee scam, was released from Dillibazar Prison in Kathmandu on Thursday morning (November 12). Onlinekhabar, citing a prison source, reported that he was handed over to his wife and other family members.

## Why he was released early

The Kathmandu District Court had sentenced Rayamajhi to four years in prison and a fine of Rs 40,000. Under Nepali law, prisoners aged 65 and over can receive a **25% reduction** in their sentence. According to myRepublica, Rayamajhi turned 65 on Wednesday (November 11).

After he applied, the court issued a revised prison warrant cutting his term by one year, to three years. Dillibazar Prison jailer Baburam Aryal had said the warrant set Thursday as his release date. SagarPari first reported the decision on November 3.

Rayamajhi was arrested in May 2023 and had been in custody since then. myRepublica reported that he spent about three and a half years behind bars. The source reports did not include any comment from him after his release.

## What the scam was

According to the Kathmandu Post, Nepalis who wanted to move to the United States were falsely promised that they would be registered as Bhutanese refugees eligible for resettlement in a third country. Forged government documents and fake refugee identities were used, and millions of rupees were collected from victims. It is considered one of Nepal's biggest criminal scandals and involved political leaders and senior officials.

The court convicted Rayamajhi of fraud, organized crime and offenses against the state. The Kathmandu Post reports that the full verdict also ordered him to repay Rs 4 million to victims. Eight defendants were ordered to return a combined Rs 153.8 million to 59 victims.

## Why it matters to Nepalis in the US

The case is a well-known example of fraud sold as a way to "get to America." US refugee resettlement, visas and green cards cannot be arranged through a paid shortcut or a middleman. If someone makes such an offer, check information only on official US government websites such as uscis.gov and travel.state.gov.
