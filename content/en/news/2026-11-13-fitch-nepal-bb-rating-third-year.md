Fitch Ratings, the international credit rating agency, has kept Nepal's sovereign credit rating at 'BB-' for a third year. The rating has stayed the same since Nepal received its first-ever sovereign rating in 2024.

In its latest report, Fitch said the rating is supported by the government's low public and external debt — most of it borrowed on highly concessional terms — strong external liquidity and access to multilateral financing. It also pointed to medium-term growth prospects from ongoing investment in hydropower.

## Debt is rising, but risk is still 'low'

According to Nepal's Public Debt Management Office, public debt crossed Rs 3 trillion (Rs 3,000 billion) for the first time in the first quarter of the current fiscal year 2026-27. Total outstanding debt reached Rs 3,009 billion, or 45.59 percent of gross domestic product (GDP). In fiscal year 2016-17 it was only Rs 697.68 billion, or 26.84 percent of GDP.

Even so, the World Bank and the International Monetary Fund (IMF) still rate Nepal's risk of debt distress as "low." Fitch also noted that Nepal's external debt is highly concessional and that support from official creditors is likely to remain strong. The agency said this structure helped preserve fiscal flexibility through shocks such as the September 2025 protests and the August 2026 floods, and that Nepal's successful completion of an IMF program in June 2026 added further support.

## Floods slow growth, with a rebound expected next year

Because of the devastating August 26 floods, Fitch projects Nepal's economic growth will slow to 3.4 percent in the current fiscal year 2026-27, down from 3.9 percent in fiscal year 2025-26. According to the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority, more than 1,400 people died in the floods and more than 5,000 remain missing, while the World Bank estimates physical damage at about $1.66 billion. Fitch said the floods affected more than 10 percent of the country's installed power capacity.

The agency expects growth to rise to 5.2 percent in fiscal year 2027-28 as reconstruction speeds up and hydropower projects and transmission lines come back online. But it warned that delays in rebuilding, or a prolonged conflict linked to Iran, could hurt imports of energy and fertilizer.

## The same weaknesses remain

On the factors holding the rating back, Fitch pointed to Nepal's underdeveloped economy, its vulnerability to external shocks and natural disasters, and per-capita income and governance indicators that are weak compared with other 'BB' countries. The report noted that the ruling Rastriya Swatantra Party's clear majority in the March election creates an opportunity for policy continuity and economic reform, but said balancing post-flood relief with reform will be a challenge.

For Nepalis in the U.S. and elsewhere who send money home, this rating does not directly affect day-to-day costs. But it is a signal of the country's economic credibility, which shapes foreign investment, borrowing costs and overall economic stability.
