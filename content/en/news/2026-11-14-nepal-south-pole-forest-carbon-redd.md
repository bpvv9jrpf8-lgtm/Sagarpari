Nepal's government has signed an agreement with South Pole, an international climate solutions company, to develop a carbon program covering more than 2.5 million hectares of forest in Koshi, Karnali and Sudurpaschim provinces. The Ministry of Forests and Environment and South Pole signed the deal on Saturday, Nov. 14, in Antalya, Türkiye, according to Nepal's state news agency RSS. Antalya is the host city of the UN climate conference, COP-31.

## What the program is

Under the agreement, the three provinces will get a jurisdictional forest carbon program, known as J-REDD+. Instead of treating one small project at a time, a jurisdictional program covers the forests of an entire province or large region as a single unit.

The program has three main goals:

- Cut greenhouse gas emissions from deforestation and forest degradation
- Promote sustainable forest management
- Improve control of forest fires

It will follow the TREES standard of the Architecture for REDD+ Transactions (ART). The forest carbon credits it produces can be sold on the international market, in line with CORSIA, the international aviation industry's carbon offsetting scheme, and Article 6 of the Paris Agreement.

## Who does what

Nepal's government will set up the policy and regulatory framework. That includes defining rights over carbon and land, setting safeguard standards, creating a benefit-sharing mechanism, and making sure local communities give free, prior and informed consent (FPIC).

South Pole will support the program's technical design, structure the investment, help raise blended finance and manage access to international carbon credit markets.

## What we don't know yet

The public details of the agreement do not name the signatories. They also do not say when the program starts, how many credits it could generate, how much money it might bring in, or what share would go to community forest user groups. For community forest users, the benefit-sharing rules and the consent process will be the parts of this program that matter most, so the regulations the government writes will deserve close attention.

For Nepalis abroad, the deal is worth watching: if it works, Nepal's forest conservation could start bringing in money through climate finance. At COP-31, Nepal also called for climate finance that is simple, predictable and grant-based.
