Foreign students studying dentistry in the US may get more time to work after graduation. On November 9 the American Dental Association (ADA) signed a joint letter asking the Department of Homeland Security to add dentistry to its list of designated science, technology, engineering and math (STEM) degrees.

The letter is addressed to US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS). It was led by the California Dental Association and also signed by the American Dental Education Association and the American Student Dental Association.

Under current rules, most foreign dental graduates on F-1 student visas can work for up to 12 months on Optional Practical Training (OPT) after finishing school. If dentistry is added to the STEM list, eligible graduates could apply for a 24-month STEM OPT extension — a total of three years of work authorization before they need H-1B sponsorship.

The letter argues that 12 months is too short for graduates and employers. Hiring paperwork, licensing verification and preparing visa sponsorship are hard to finish in that time, it says, noting that students, recent graduates and employers have found 12 months insufficient for both employment and sponsorship filings.

The groups also argue that dentistry is itself a scientific and technical field, built on biological and physical sciences, modern imaging, computer-aided design and manufacturing, materials science and biomechanics. The letter notes that 13 health-related degree codes in the 51 series already qualify for the STEM benefit.

A shortage of dentists in rural and underserved communities is another key argument. Federally qualified health centers are a main source of dental care in rural and remote areas, but they struggle to complete sponsorship within the short OPT window, the letter says. The short timeline pushes graduates toward employers who can process paperwork quickly rather than toward the communities that need them most.

The ADA clarified that it has no separate policy on STEM designation or OPT extensions, but has broader policy on internationally trained and US-trained dental professionals.

For now this is only a request; no rule has changed. Dental graduates will get STEM OPT only after USCIS and DHS update the list. For Nepali students thinking about studying dentistry in the US, approval would make the path to work and experience after graduation much easier.
