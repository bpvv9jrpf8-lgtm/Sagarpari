Nepal will not take the Rs 184 billion concessional line of credit India announced during Prime Minister Balen Shah's visit all at once. Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle told the House of Representatives' Finance Committee on Tuesday that the money will be drawn gradually, based on what individual projects need and how far they have progressed.

Answering lawmakers' questions about the agreements reached in New Delhi, Wagle said the visit brought Nepal a Rs 184 billion credit line and a Rs 56 billion grant. Together, those make up the roughly Rs 240 billion package India announced.

## Interest and repayment still open

Key terms have not been settled. According to Onlinekhabar, technical negotiations between the two countries are still needed to fix the interest rate, the repayment period and the grace period before repayments begin.

Wagle pointed to the option of borrowing and repaying in Indian rupees. He argued that this would carry less foreign-exchange risk than a loan in US dollars. That option has not been finalized.

## Energy and transmission lines first

Wagle said the credit could be especially useful for energy infrastructure, including transmission lines. He said the facility was arranged with Nepal's potential to generate about 40,000 megawatts of electricity in mind.

He acknowledged that earlier Indian credit lines used for road building ran into difficulties because of conditions on construction materials and contractors. The new facility, he said, could be used more effectively in the energy sector.

## Air routes called a diplomatic win

Wagle also described the agreement on two-way air routes for Gautam Buddha International Airport in Bhairahawa as an important diplomatic achievement of the visit. He said the government is also pursuing additional air routes via Mahendranagar and Janakpur.

Prime Minister Shah has returned home after his two-day state visit to India. Lawmakers have now started pressing the government on how the aid and agreements from the trip will be put into practice.
