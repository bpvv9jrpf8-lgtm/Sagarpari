Fewer international students are coming to the United States, but the number of students from Nepal has reached a record. The Institute of International Education's annual Open Doors 2026 report, released November 16, says nine of the top 25 countries sending students to the US hit all-time highs in the 2025/26 school year, and Nepal is one of them.

The nine are Bangladesh, Canada, Colombia, Ghana, Italy, Nepal, Pakistan, Peru and Vietnam.

## Overall numbers are down

The wider picture looks different. US colleges and universities hosted 1,146,163 international students in 2025/26, down 3% from the year before. First-time enrollment fell 18% to 228,123.

Graduate enrollment dropped 13% to 423,078. Master's students fell 22%, while doctoral students rose 3%. Undergraduate numbers barely moved, slipping 0.3% to 356,165.

Optional Practical Training (OPT), the work period after graduation, grew 14% to 335,785 students. Of those, 58% were in their first post-completion year and 42% were on STEM OPT extensions. India stayed the top sender for a third straight year with 339,435 students, followed by China with 254,458.

## A steeper drop this fall

IIE's Fall 2026 Snapshot, based on 809 institutions, shows total international enrollment down 9% this fall and new enrollment down 15%. Graduate numbers fell 15%, undergraduate 3% and OPT 6%.

Among schools reporting declines, 88% pointed to the visa application process, 70% to travel restrictions, 60% to students choosing other countries and 45% to costs. For graduate recruitment, schools named India (68%), Vietnam (50%) and Nepal (41%) as top focus countries.

## More than 33,000 Nepalis in October

Inside Higher Ed reported Monday that an analysis of government SEVIS data by the Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration found more than 33,000 students from Nepal enrolled in US colleges in October, up nearly 15% since 2024. In that analysis of students from 26 countries hit by political upheaval or natural disasters, Nepal was the largest source country.

## What it means for Nepali students

Nepali enrollment is growing even as tougher visa processing and new rules pull overall international numbers down. Students planning to come to the US, and those already here, should check regularly with their school's international student office (DSO) about visa interviews, OPT and CPT rules, and recent court decisions.
