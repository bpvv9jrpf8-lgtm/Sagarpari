There is major relief for the hundreds of thousands of international students in the United States. On Monday, a federal court in the District of Massachusetts struck down in full a Department of Homeland Security (DHS) rule that would have set fixed limits on how long international students and exchange visitors can stay. Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV found the rule arbitrary and unlawful and vacated it under the Administrative Procedure Act.

The rule would have ended "duration of status," or D/S, a system in place for decades. Under D/S, F-1 students and J-1 exchange visitors can stay in the U.S. for as long as their program lasts. The new rule would have limited admission to the program end date on the student's documents, up to a maximum of four years, required an extension application to USCIS for more time, and restricted things like changing educational objectives, transferring schools and starting new programs.

The court found that DHS did not adequately consider less burdensome alternatives, such as existing monitoring and enforcement tools like SEVIS. The ruling also says the limits on academic mobility went beyond the department's legal authority. The case was brought by a coalition of higher-education and labor groups, including NAFSA and the Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration. The rule had been set to take effect on Sept. 15, but the court blocked it with a preliminary order on Sept. 14. Monday's ruling is the final judgment in the case.

After the ruling, the old D/S framework remains in place. F-1 students and J-1 exchange visitors will continue to be admitted for the length of their studies. Procedures for extending I-20s and DS-2019s, F-1 OPT and STEM OPT, and J-1 academic training stay the same, and no extension of stay application to USCIS is needed. According to Yale University's international students office, students and scholars do not need to take any action after the ruling. The Foundation for India and Indian Diaspora Studies (FIIDS) welcomed the decision, saying it brings major relief to thousands of international students, researchers and their families.

The government can appeal the ruling — NAFSA says the government had already appealed the earlier preliminary order. So the appeal remains to be seen. For now, Nepali students in bachelor's, master's and doctoral programs in the U.S. can be sure their status and programs will continue under the old rules.
