The number of Nepali students at American colleges has reached a new high. According to the annual Open Doors 2026 report, released November 16 by the Institute of International Education (IIE), 29,058 students from Nepal studied at U.S. colleges and universities in the 2025/26 academic year. That is up 16.7% from 24,890 the year before.

Nepal ranks fifth among countries sending students to the United States. The report, sponsored by the U.S. Department of State, says nine of the top 25 sending countries reached all-time highs this year, and Nepal is one of them.

## By level of study

According to IIE's fact sheet on Nepal:

- **Undergraduate:** 16,082 students, up 27.2%
- **Graduate:** 8,645 students, up 3.5%
- **On OPT:** 4,152 students, up 10.9%
- **Non-degree:** 179 students

About 83.4% of Nepali students attend public institutions, and roughly 22.6% are at associate-degree institutions, mostly community colleges. The top host states are Texas, Ohio, New York, Missouri and Louisiana. The U.S. Department of Commerce estimates that Nepali students contributed about $1.075 billion to the U.S. economy in 2025.

## New visas and enrollment are falling

The record total may not last. The same fact sheet shows the number of F (student) visas issued to Nepalis down 53.3% and J visas down 50.3%.

Nationally, 1,146,163 international students were in the U.S. in 2025/26, a 3% decline. First-time enrollment fell 18% to 228,123, while students on OPT rose 14% to 335,785. India (339,435) and China (254,458) remain first and second.

A separate fall 2026 snapshot of 809 institutions shows a steeper drop: total international students down 9% and new enrollment down 15%. Among schools reporting declines, 88% cited the visa application process and 70% cited travel restrictions. Still, 41% of institutions said Nepal is a focus of their graduate recruitment.

Students and families planning to study in the U.S. should prepare early for visa interviews, costs and college choices, and rely on official sources.
