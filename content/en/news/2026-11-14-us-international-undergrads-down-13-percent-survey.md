New international undergraduate enrollment at U.S. universities fell **13%** this year compared with last year, according to a global survey released Thursday, Nov. 12. It's a signal worth watching for Nepali students and families planning study in the United States.

The **Global Enrolment Benchmark Survey** was run jointly by student recruitment platform **Studyportals**, the U.S. international education association **NAFSA**, and **Oxford Test of English**. It covered **437 universities in 63 countries**.

## What the survey found for the US

- New international undergraduates were **down 13%** (per Reuters).
- At the master's level, **59%** of U.S. institutions reported fewer international students, up from 48% in 2025 (per ICEF Monitor).
- **37%** of U.S. institutions expect enrollment to fall again in fall 2027.
- **90%** of U.S. institutions named restrictive government policies as the main barrier to recruiting international students.

Reuters reported that visa processing delays, tougher screening and changes to post-study work rules led many students to defer or drop their plans. For the U.S., it cited expanded social media checks, thousands of revoked visas and tighter limits on length of stay.

## Elsewhere

The UK saw an even steeper drop, with new international undergraduates down **19%**. Universities in Asia and continental Europe, by contrast, reported growth: 59% of Asian institutions said they enrolled more international master's students. Canada appears to be recovering somewhat after last year's sharp decline.

NAFSA Executive Director and CEO Dr. Fanta Aw said the results show that "national policies matter to international students making major decisions about where to study."

## What it means for Nepali students

The survey does not break results down by students' home country, so it does not show how many fewer students came from Nepal. It does show that U.S. visa and policy uncertainty is shaping decisions for students everywhere.

One caveat: the survey was conducted before the Nov. 3 U.S. midterm elections. Studyportals' own data show views of U.S. on-campus program pages rose 40% between Nov. 1 and Nov. 8. CEO Edwin van Rest said the challenge now is turning that interest into applications, visas and enrollments.

If you are preparing to study in the U.S., always check official guidance at [travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/study/student-visa.html) and your university's international student office.
