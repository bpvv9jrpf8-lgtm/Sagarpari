Immigrants fighting removal (deportation) cases in the U.S. now have far less time to make their case. On Monday, November 23, the Justice Department's Executive Office for Immigration Review (EOIR), which runs the immigration courts, issued a new policy memo (PM 27-02) that changes how cases move through the courts. Instead of months, people will now get just 10 days to file written pleadings and applications for relief.

## How the new process works

Under the new model, in cases where the immigrant has a lawyer, the first hearing (the master calendar hearing) will be canceled if the attorney files Form EOIR-28 at least 15 days before it. The court will instead send a scheduling order, and written pleadings and evidence on the removal charges will be due within 10 days of that order. Under the earlier Biden-era guidance, the same step allowed a month after the judge's scheduling order.

After pleadings are filed, the other side gets 20 days to respond. The judge will then decide whether removability has been established, ask for more briefing or evidence, or set a hearing. If removability is established, the deadline to file applications for relief or protection, with supporting evidence, will also be just 10 days from the order. Previously, 60 days were allowed for those applications.

## Missing a deadline has serious consequences

If written pleadings are not filed on time, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) can ask the judge to find the person removable and issue a final order. Applications filed after the deadline will be treated as abandoned. Once a case is ready, the main (merits) hearing will be scheduled within 90 days.

The new rules apply to cases started on or after November 23. Older cases will be moved into the new model only after separate decisions. EOIR says the goal is to reduce the backlog and cut the number of hearings held for routine early steps. Judges can still extend deadlines on a written motion.

## Second tightening in a week

The change follows another strict rule last week. A final rule published November 17 cuts the deadline to appeal an immigration judge's decision from 30 days to 10 days, effective December 17. SagarPari reported on that rule earlier.

For Nepalis in the U.S. who are in removal proceedings, this change has a direct impact. Anyone with a case should contact a lawyer quickly so pleadings and applications can be prepared within the 10-day window. Missing it could mean losing the right to apply at all.
