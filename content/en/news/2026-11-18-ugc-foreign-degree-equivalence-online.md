Nepali students who earned degrees from foreign universities, including in the United States, no longer have to visit an office in Kathmandu to get an equivalence certificate. Nepal's University Grants Commission (UGC) began issuing the certificates through an online system in July 2026.

That is according to a three-month progress report the UGC submitted to Education and Sports Minister Sasmit Pokharel, The Kathmandu Post reported on Wednesday, November 18. The report says the process has been fully digitized to make the service easier, more transparent and quicker.

## Why equivalence matters

For anyone who studied abroad and wants to continue studying, apply for scholarships, practice a profession or work in Nepal, an equivalence certificate is a key document. It is especially important in regulated health professions that require a license.

## From TU to the UGC

Tribhuvan University (TU) used to handle equivalence. After amendments to the UGC Act last year, the responsibility moved to the UGC, which is now authorized to decide equivalence, credit transfers and quality assurance.

The regulations needed for that work, however, have still not been approved. The UGC sent a draft of the equivalence regulations to the Education Ministry on October 2. Until they are approved, equivalence is being decided under TU's existing rules.

Before the online system, the UGC accepted applications but could not issue permanent certificates. More than 1,000 applicants were left waiting, including health graduates who needed recognition to get licensed. They were given temporary letters.

UGC Chairperson Khadga Bahadur KC says the process is now faster and easier. The Post's report does not give the portal address, fees, required documents or processing time, so applicants should check the UGC's official website for current details before applying.

## Other higher education reforms

The progress report also lays out other plans:

- **UGC Act amendments:** The 1993 act is being revised to fit Nepal's federal structure and the growth of provincial universities. A committee led by former UGC chair Bhim Prasad Subedi will advise the government.
- **Autonomy for Prithvi Narayan Campus:** The UGC is discussing autonomy and degree-awarding powers for the campus in Pokhara. Plans to make select colleges autonomous have gone unimplemented for more than a decade.
- **PhD standards:** A committee coordinated by Shankar Khanal, dean of TU's Institute of Science and Technology, will review doctoral standards. PhD programs now follow minimum standards introduced in 2016.
- **Journal rankings:** The UGC will resume classifying Nepali research journals, with a committee led by Narayan Adhikari, head of TU's Central Department of Physics.
- **Teacher eligibility test:** Preparations are under way for a National Eligibility Test (NET). Once it is introduced, all higher education teachers will have to pass it.
