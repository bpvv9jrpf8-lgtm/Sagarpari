Tribhuvan University (TU), Nepal's oldest and largest university, is preparing to bring the semester system to all of its faculties and academic programs. The plan is part of an effort to raise the quality of higher education.

## Talks with the ministry

Nepal's Ministry of Education and Sports met TU officials on Friday, November 13, to discuss academic standards, the examination system and the higher education sector as a whole. According to the secretariat of Education and Sports Minister Sasmit Pokharel, the discussion covered:

- strengthening TU's institutional reputation and promoting research
- improving teaching and learning
- setting student quotas based on each institution's academic capacity
- launching new academic programs
- making college affiliation more systematic and criteria-based
- increasing investment in higher education

TU officials said curricula are being developed to implement the semester system, alongside policy reforms and curriculum revisions. No start date has been announced.

## "Quality before numbers"

Shailendra Jha, an adviser to the minister, pushed for an electronic attendance system. He said improving quality should come before adding more affiliated colleges, and that every affiliated institution needs adequate infrastructure, libraries and laboratories.

TU Vice-Chancellor Prof. Dr. Bhola Thapa said the university is committed to raising academic standards and becoming a "centre of excellence." He pledged to work with the ministry on a comprehensive plan to reform higher education.

The meeting also discussed reclaiming TU's land and preparing a detailed inventory of university property.

## Why it matters abroad

Many Nepali students who come to the United States and other countries for higher education earned their undergraduate degrees at TU. Changes to how TU teaches and examines students are closely watched by students preparing to study abroad and their families. Details on when and how the change will take effect have yet to be decided. SagarPari will update this story as more information becomes available.
