Three Nepali nationals were among 11 people injured on Tuesday, Nov. 24, when a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District was hit in an attack that Saudi Arabia's General Directorate of Civil Defense blamed on Yemen's Houthi rebels.

## Who was hurt

According to Arab News and Asharq Al-Awsat, citing the Saudi Press Agency, two of the injured are Saudi citizens. The other nine are foreign residents of the kingdom: three Nepalis, two Indians and four Egyptians.

The injured have not been named, and the Saudi statements did not say how serious their injuries are. As of this writing, there was no official word from Nepal's embassy in Riyadh or the Ministry of Foreign Affairs on the condition of the Nepali workers.

Civil Defense said authorities responded immediately and took the necessary steps to protect lives and property.

## A drone strike

Maj. Gen. Turki Al-Malki, spokesman for the Saudi-led coalition in Yemen, said the attack was carried out with a drone, Arab News reported. He warned that the deliberate targeting of civilians and civilian sites "will be met with a firm response." The coalition called it a "cowardly terrorist attack" and said an on-site investigation was underway.

The coalition has not named the building that was struck. Al Jazeera reported it was the second attack on Riyadh's financial district in less than a week; an earlier strike on Nov. 19 caused material damage only.

## Why it matters for Nepalis

Saudi Arabia is one of the main destinations for Nepali migrant workers. Houthi drone and missile attacks on Saudi targets have increased in recent weeks. In October, a Nepali worker was killed in a drone attack on a Saudi oil field, after which Nepal's embassy in Riyadh urged Nepalis to stay indoors during security alerts and avoid going out unnecessarily.

Families worried about relatives in Saudi Arabia can contact Nepal's embassy in Riyadh for official information. SagarPari will update this story as more details become available.
