More than two weeks after the midterm elections, Alaska's Senate race finally has a winner. The Associated Press called the race for Democrat **Mary Peltola**, who defeated two-term Republican Sen. **Dan Sullivan**.

The result gives Democrats a **56-44 majority** in the U.S. Senate, according to the AP. Democrats flipped nine Republican-held seats this cycle.

## 50.1% — no ranked-choice count needed

With an estimated 99% of the vote counted, Peltola has **50.1%** of first-choice votes, Alaska Public Media reported. Because she cleared a majority, Alaska's ranked-choice tabulation is not needed. The AP said Sullivan has no viable path to reelection.

Overseas ballots can still arrive through Wednesday, and the state is scheduled to certify results on **Nov. 25**, according to Alaska Public Media. Members of the new Congress are sworn in on Jan. 3.

## First Alaska Native in the Senate

Peltola previously held Alaska's only seat in the U.S. House, becoming the first Alaska Native to serve in Congress. She lost her bid for a second full term to Republican Nick Begich in 2024. She will now be the first Alaska Native to serve in the U.S. Senate, and the first Democrat to win a Senate race in Alaska since 2008.

"The status quo in D.C. is not working for Alaska," Peltola said in a statement, according to the AP.

Sullivan, a Marine veteran and former Alaska attorney general, was first elected to the Senate in 2014. No concession had been reported. His spokesperson, Nate Adams, did not respond to the AP's request for comment; he had earlier said the campaign would let the process run until every legal vote is counted and certified.

## A close race that went to court

Early in the count, only a few hundred votes separated the two candidates, and Peltola's campaign went to court seeking records of rejected ballots. Alaska's U.S. House race has not been called yet: Begich has 47.5% and Bill Hill 41.8%.

Because the Senate passes federal laws and confirms judges and Cabinet officials, which party holds the majority shapes policy on issues including immigration.
