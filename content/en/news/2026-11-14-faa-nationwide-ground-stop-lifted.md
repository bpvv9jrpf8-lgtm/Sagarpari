The Federal Aviation Administration ordered a nationwide ground stop at U.S. airports on Friday afternoon, Nov. 13, after a communications problem hit the country's air traffic control centers. The FAA lifted the hours-long stop at 8:15 p.m. Eastern, Engadget reported.

For Nepalis planning trips within the U.S. or to Nepal as the holiday season approaches, the knock-on effects of canceled and delayed flights may linger for a while even though departures have resumed.

## What happened

The FAA said flights were halted "due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety." According to the Associated Press, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said there was a communications issue affecting the 22 regional air traffic control centers that guide planes en route.

Engadget reported that Duffy blamed an "incredibly old" air traffic control system. The U.S. still uses TDM, a legacy analog technology, to move information between facilities, and Duffy said its routers were overloaded with data. Equipment that has already been replaced was not affected. Not all communications and radar were lost, but flights were grounded out of "an abundance of caution."

An FAA advisory said international flights would be "managed on a case-by-case basis."

## How many flights were hit

Al Jazeera and Reuters reported that about 7,000 flights had been delayed or canceled during the stop, according to the flight-tracking site FlightAware. Engadget said hundreds of flights were canceled and thousands of planes already in the air were diverted.

## Was it a cyberattack?

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was "no indication" of a cyberattack, according to Engadget, but added that officials were still looking and would not rule it out.

The AP said it was the first nationwide ground stop since January 2023, when a key FAA computer system failed. Before that, the last one followed the Sept. 11, 2001, attacks.

## Push for more funding

Congress approved $12.5 billion last year to overhaul the air traffic control system, the AP reported. Work is underway to replace copper wiring with fiber and to swap out radios. Duffy says about $30 billion more is needed, and the White House has begun lobbying Congress for it.
