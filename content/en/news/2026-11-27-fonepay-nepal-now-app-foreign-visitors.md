Foreign visitors to Nepal can now pay the way locals do — by scanning a QR code at a shop, restaurant or hotel. Digital payment network **Fonepay**, in partnership with **Nabil Bank**, has launched **Nepal Now**, a payment app built for international travelers. The launch was reported on Thursday, Nov. 26.

## How it works

According to The Kathmandu Post and NepaliTelecom:

- The app is available on the **App Store and Google Play**.
- To register, you need a **valid passport and a Nepal visa**.
- You load money with an international **Visa, Mastercard or UnionPay** card.
- You can then pay by scanning QR codes at **more than 2 million Fonepay merchant locations** across Nepal.

QR payments are everywhere in Nepal, from small tea shops to local hotels, but until now they generally required a Nepali bank account or wallet. That left many tourists relying on cash or hunting for places that accept cards.

Fonepay CEO **Diwas Kumar** said the app extends Nepal's digital payment system to international travelers. "Our aim is to make everyday payments simpler for travellers," he told The Kathmandu Post.

## What it means for Nepalis in the US

The app could be handy for people of Nepali origin who travel home on a U.S. or other foreign passport with a Nepal visa, and for American friends and family visiting Nepal, since it is reported to accept cards issued abroad.

A few things to keep in mind:

- The sources do not list **fees, exchange rates, transaction limits**, or whether unused balances can be refunded. Check with your own bank whether your card charges a foreign transaction fee.
- The sources also do not say whether **NRN card holders** or visa-exempt travelers can sign up. Check Fonepay's or Nabil Bank's official information before relying on the app.

NepaliTelecom notes that Fonepay also helped launch Nepal-India cross-border QR payments in 2023.
