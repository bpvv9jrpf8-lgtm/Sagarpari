Nepali football has moved a step closer to returning to international play. Nepal's **National Sports Council (NSC)** on Thursday, November 19, lifted **all suspensions and restrictions, administrative and financial,** that it had imposed on the All Nepal Football Association (ANFA) at various times.

According to Onlinekhabar English, the NSC executive committee made the decision under Section 29(3) of the National Sports Development Act, 2077 BS (2020). The restrictions are lifted from the date the letter was issued. The letter, signed by NSC member secretary Ram Charitra Mehta, was sent to ANFA, and world governing body **FIFA** and the Asian Football Confederation (AFC) were informed.

The Kathmandu Post reports that the decision followed a meeting the same day in Bangkok, Thailand, held on the occasion of the 30th AFC Awards. Education and Sports Minister Sasmit Pokharel, FIFA and AFC officials, the minister's adviser Siddhi Byanjankar and NSC vice chair Bishal Shrestha took part. ANFA spokesperson Suresh Shah confirmed the association had received the NSC letter.

## How the dispute unfolded

- **March 25:** The NSC suspended ANFA for three months, opposing ANFA's plan to hold early elections in Jhapa.
- **May 15:** After 51 days, the NSC revoked the suspension but attached conditions, including tier-wise elections, and ANFA remained barred from financial and other activities.
- **June 24:** FIFA and the AFC suspended Nepal indefinitely, citing third-party interference. According to HimalPress, the suspension kept Nepal out of the SAFF Championship held this month in Dhaka.

## What happens next

The Kathmandu Post says the early election process in Jhapa has been scrapped. A ministry official, speaking to Kantipur on condition of anonymity, said ANFA under president Pankaj Bikram Nembang will now have to hold tier-wise elections. Once ANFA starts that process, the official expects FIFA and the AFC to review the case and lift the suspension within about a month. FIFA has not officially announced a date.

The Post describes the outcome as a compromise by both sides. FIFA had demanded that Nembang be allowed to work without government interference. Nepali football fans abroad are now watching for FIFA's final decision.
