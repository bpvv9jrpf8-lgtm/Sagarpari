A second federal court has blocked President Donald Trump's newer executive order that would deny U.S. citizenship to certain groups of children born in the country. U.S. District Judge Christopher R. Cooper in Washington, D.C., issued a preliminary injunction on Wednesday, Nov. 25.

The case is *Nathan v. Trump*. Cooper's ruling is the second against Executive Order 14418, issued in August 2026. On Sept. 2, U.S. District Judge Deborah Boardman in Maryland reached a similar decision.

## What the August order said

As quoted in the ruling, the order sought to deny citizenship mainly to children in these groups:

- A parent is designated by the U.S. as a terrorist
- A non-citizen parent works for a foreign government or for an international organization that has immunity
- Either parent took part in "birth tourism" — traveling to the U.S. to give birth
- The child is born in a U.S. territory where federal law does not grant citizenship, such as American Samoa

## What the court said

Cooper certified a class made up of children who are or will be unlawfully denied citizenship under the order, and barred the government from enforcing the order against them.

"As the Supreme Court has made clear, however, the President cannot carve out new exceptions to birthright citizenship for children born within the United States and subject to its laws," he wrote.

## Background

Trump first moved to limit birthright citizenship with an order (14160) signed on Jan. 20, 2025. It covered children of parents in the country unlawfully or on temporary visas. In June 2026, the Supreme Court struck that order down 6–3 in *Trump v. Barbara*. In August, the administration issued a second order aimed at new, narrower groups.

## What it means

The constitutional rule that children born in the United States are U.S. citizens remains in place. With two courts blocking it, the August order is not currently in effect. The ruling is preliminary, and the government can appeal. We will report further rulings.
