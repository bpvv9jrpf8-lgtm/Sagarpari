The **U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit** has ruled that immigrants who entered the country without inspection have **no constitutional right to a bond hearing** while held in ICE custody. The ruling applies in **Texas, Louisiana and Mississippi**.

The full court, sitting en banc, issued the decision on Friday, **October 23**, by a **10-7** vote. Judge **Cory Wilson** wrote the majority opinion.

## What the court decided

The court held that people detained under federal immigration law (8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A)) are not entitled to an individual bond hearing on whether they are dangerous or a flight risk. It also said the length of their detention does not by itself raise a constitutional problem.

The majority wrote that detainees can end their detention "at any moment by agreeing to leave the country." In dissent, Judge **Leslie Southwick** wrote that the ruling builds an "impenetrable wall between the Constitution and these long-term residents."

The decision overturns a July ruling by a three-judge panel of the same court, which had said people held longer than 90 days must get a hearing.

## The case

Three men arrested during routine traffic stops in Texas brought the case. According to *The Texas Tribune*, each had lived in the U.S. for at least 14 years and has U.S.-citizen children. District judges had found that holding them without a bond hearing was unlawful and ordered them released. The appeals court has now vacated those orders.

Since July 2025, the Trump administration has held that anyone who entered without inspection can be detained without bond, no matter how many years they have lived in the U.S. Thousands of lawsuits have challenged that policy.

## What comes next

- The ruling is binding only in Texas, Louisiana and Mississippi. *The Texas Tribune* reports that the administration has moved many detainees from other states into Texas facilities.
- Some other federal appeals courts have ruled for immigrants on this question, so the rules now differ by region.
- On **October 1**, the **U.S. Supreme Court** agreed to hear a separate case on the mandatory detention policy. No argument date has been set.
- According to *The Texas Tribune*, the ruling does not bar habeas petitions from people who entered the country legally.

## What Nepalis should know

The decision matters most for people who entered without inspection and have cases pending in immigration court. How it affects you can depend on how you entered, for example with or without a visa. Keep your documents safe, give your family a lawyer's phone number, and seek legal help right away if you are detained.
