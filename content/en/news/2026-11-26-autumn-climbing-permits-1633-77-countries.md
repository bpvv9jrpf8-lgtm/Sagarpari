The number of climbers permitted on Nepal's mountains this autumn has reached **1,633**. According to the Department of Tourism, climbers from **77 countries**, organized into **190 teams**, received permits for **50 peaks**.

Of them, **1,253 are men** and **380 are women**. Since the last week of August the department has collected **Rs 334.28 million** (about $2.19 million) in royalties, said department information officer **Suraj Ghimire**.

As of Oct. 28, 1,410 climbers from 76 countries had taken permits for 44 peaks — so 223 more climbers and six more peaks have been added since.

## Manaslu leads again

**Manaslu (8,163 m)**, the world's eighth-highest mountain, was again the most popular:

- **Manaslu:** 500 climbers in 43 teams, Rs 225.85 million in royalties
- **Ama Dablam:** 476 climbers in 40 teams, Rs 71.34 million
- **Himlung Himal:** 130 climbers in 13 teams, Rs 7.97 million
- **Dhaulagiri I:** 37 climbers in six teams
- **Makalu:** 3 climbers

Permits were also issued for Putha Hiunchuli, Baruntse, Gangapurna West, Annapurna IV, Chamlang, Pumori, Saribung, Thorong Peak and Lamjung Himal, among others.

## 130 climbers from the U.S.

**France** (150) sent the most climbers, followed by **China** (146). The **United States, with 130**, was third, ahead of Russia (124), the United Kingdom (112), Germany (102) and Nepal (90). Spain, Switzerland, Australia and the Czech Republic also sent sizable numbers.

## Why it matters

Autumn is Nepal's second main climbing season after spring. Beyond royalties for the government, expeditions bring jobs and income to guides, porters, hotels and transport businesses. Manaslu, Ama Dablam and Himlung alone account for about 91 percent of the royalties collected so far.
