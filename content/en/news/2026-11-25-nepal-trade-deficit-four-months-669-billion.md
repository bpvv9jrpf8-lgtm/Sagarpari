Nepal's foreign trade grew by about a third in the first four months of the current fiscal year (mid-July to mid-November). Data released Wednesday by the Department of Customs show total trade of Rs 937.24 billion, up 33.33% from Rs 702.95 billion in the same period last year.

Exports grew faster than imports, but because imports are so much larger, the trade deficit kept widening.

## The key numbers

- **Imports:** Rs 803.10 billion, up 31.77% from Rs 609.45 billion
- **Exports:** Rs 134.15 billion, up 43.48% from Rs 93.49 billion
- **Trade deficit:** Rs 668.95 billion, up 29.65% from Rs 515.95 billion

Exports make up only 14.31% of total trade, against 85.69% for imports. According to HimalPress, Nepal now buys about Rs 5.99 worth of goods from abroad for every Rs 1 it sells, down from 6.52 a year earlier.

## Soybean oil tops both lists

Soybean oil leads both imports and exports. Nepal News reports that Nepal imported Rs 62.81 billion of crude soybean oil in the four months, and exported Rs 61.35 billion of processed soybean oil — 45.73% of all exports.

The next biggest exports were carpets (Rs 3.89 billion), large cardamom (Rs 3.44 billion) and jute (Rs 3.31 billion). Petroleum products are a major share of imports: fuel, including aviation fuel, alone cost Rs 136.71 billion, about 17% of the import bill.

## India accounts for most of the gap

Trade with India made up 61.32% of Nepal's total, or Rs 574.80 billion. Nepal ran a deficit of Rs 349.49 billion with India and Rs 163.26 billion with China. Of the 138 countries Nepal traded with, it had a deficit with 104 and a surplus with 34.

Birgunj remained the busiest customs point, handling 48.49% of imports and 40.37% of exports.
