Nepal and India are preparing to unveil a joint 10-point vision document for their partnership during Prime Minister Balen Shah's upcoming visit to India. The Kathmandu Post, citing a minister in the Nepali government, reported that the document is in its final stages.

The vision is built on the idea of "Samriddha Nepal, Samunnat Bharat" — Prosperous Nepal, Developed India. Its goal is to turn existing bilateral commitments into measurable results and to set priorities and targets for the years ahead.

## Who is drafting it

According to the minister quoted by the Post, the foreign ministers of both countries are preparing the draft jointly, with input from each side's prime minister's office, foreign ministry and finance ministry. "We will set goals, priorities and targets in each specific area so that both sides can see concrete progress," the minister said, adding that economic progress and development are the document's key themes.

The Post reported that the document was discussed on Tuesday and Wednesday in meetings involving Prime Minister Shah, Foreign Minister Shisir Khanal and India's newly appointed ambassador to Nepal, Sudhakar Dalela.

## What it covers

The document is expected to cover economic partnership, connectivity, investment, water resources and energy, digital connectivity, good governance, people-to-people ties, education, health, sports, and trade and transit.

The minister pointed to energy as a model. Under a long-term power trade agreement signed in January 2024, India committed to importing 10,000 megawatts of electricity from Nepal over 10 years. Officials are working to set similar concrete targets in other areas, the minister said. In 2022, during then Prime Minister Sher Bahadur Deuba's visit, the two countries issued a Joint Vision Statement on Power Sector Cooperation.

## Visit set for Nov. 22–23

Foreign Ministry officials say Shah is scheduled to visit India on November 22 and 23, though the dates have not been officially announced. The vision document is expected to be released alongside the customary joint statement at the end of the visit.

Former Indian ambassador to Nepal Ranjit Rae, who is in Kathmandu this week, backed the initiative and said setting clear targets is just as important. He suggested the document be made public and that both governments and other stakeholders, including the media, monitor its implementation.

Earlier reports said Nepal plans to seek more than $2 billion in Indian assistance during the visit. The final text of the vision document and the official itinerary have not yet been released.
