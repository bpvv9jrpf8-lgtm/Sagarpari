President Donald Trump has acknowledged that his administration's immigration enforcement and deportation policy "might be too tough." He also said some kind of immigration deal with Democrats in the new Congress could be possible.

## What Trump said

Trump made the remarks in an interview on CBS's "60 Minutes" that aired Sunday. According to the Associated Press, the interview was recorded on Friday. It was Trump's first major interview since Republicans lost both the House and the Senate.

According to the AP, Trump said his deportation policy was one of the reasons Republicans lost the election. "I think it might be too tough, yeah," he said in the interview.

Asked about a possible deal with Democrats, he said, "The deal would be we would work something out." He also called such a deal "actually doable." But according to the AP, Trump gave no details about what that deal would look like.

According to The American Bazaar, Trump claimed he deserves good marks for removing "bad people" from the country, but said he felt bad that people with good jobs were also being removed.

## A contrast with Miller's appointment

The AP noted that shortly after the interview was recorded, Trump named Stephen Miller, the chief architect of his hardline immigration policy, as the new White House chief of staff. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer responded: "That's not a course correction. That's a promotion."

Democrats will take control of Congress in January. They have said investigations of ICE and DHS will be a priority.

## What it means for Nepalis

No new policy, rule or executive order has been announced so far. Trump's comments are only a political signal. The reports do not mention anything he said about specific changes to DACA, H-1B, green cards, TPS or refugees. Nepalis in the U.S. should not make decisions about their immigration cases based on these remarks. Any real change would be officially published by USCIS, DHS or in the Federal Register.
