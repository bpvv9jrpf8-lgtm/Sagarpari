At the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, Nepal pressed for international money for climate adaptation to flow directly to the communities that face the greatest risk. Speaking at the formal launch of a new facility under LIFE-AR, an initiative of the least developed countries, Nepal restated its commitment to putting community-led adaptation first.

Agriculture, Forests and Environment Minister Gita Chaudhary spoke at the facility's launch event on November 11. She said LIFE-AR is not just a funding channel but a chance to change how climate programs are planned, financed and carried out. She noted that Nepal contributes almost nothing to greenhouse gas emissions, yet suffers serious effects of climate change.

Chaudhary described the disaster that struck the Rasuwa–Bhotekoshi area on August 26 as a "mountain tsunami." She cited preliminary estimates of 1,457 people dead and 5,285 missing. According to an initial assessment by the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority, Nepal needs about $4.78 billion for recovery, resettlement and reconstruction after the disaster, close to 10 percent of the country's gross domestic product.

The minister said Nepal signed a memorandum of understanding with LIFE-AR on May 8 and is now working to set up the facility. She thanked the International Institute for Environment and Development for its support and backed the move to have the facility run by the least developed countries themselves. According to LIFE-AR's official page, the least developed countries group has taken full ownership of the initiative with the facility's launch. Its goals are to put all least developed countries on a climate-resilient development path by 2030 and to reach net-zero emissions by 2050.

Nepal will host LIFE-AR's 15th board meeting in Kathmandu next year. The meeting is expected to discuss how to scale up models that deliver adaptation finance at the community level.
