The Nagdhunga–Sisnekhola tunnel, which links Kathmandu with the Prithvi Highway, collected Rs16,447,000 in tolls in its first 15 days of charging fees. Tolling began on November 1.

A total of 119,029 vehicles used the tunnel during that period, [The Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/11/20/nagdhunga-tunnelway-collects-rs16-4-million-in-tolls-in-first-15-days) reported, citing the Nagdhunga Tunnel Construction Project.

## About 7,935 vehicles a day

According to the project's figures:

- **Average daily traffic:** 7,935 vehicles
- **Average daily revenue:** about Rs1.1 million
- **Busiest day:** 10,354 vehicles, more than Rs1.46 million in tolls
- **Quietest day:** 4,266 vehicles, about Rs372,000 in tolls

The project cautions that these early numbers may not reflect normal traffic, because the first two weeks fell during the festival season, when travel usually rises. An official at the project said a clearer picture would come after watching traffic from mid-November to mid-December, which would help estimate a full year's revenue.

## Rs220 million a year to run

The tunnel is operated by the Yusin-ART joint venture under a five-year contract. Operating costs are estimated at about Rs1.1 billion over five years, or roughly Rs220 million a year.

The operator must deposit toll money into the Road Board Nepal account every day. Except on public holidays, bank staff come to the tunnel at 3 p.m. to collect the cash. Payments made by QR code go straight to the Road Board account.

## Fees and time saved

Cars and vans pay Rs65 entering Kathmandu and Rs60 leaving. Buses and trucks pay Rs260 and Rs200, and larger trucks and heavy vehicles pay Rs600 and Rs250. Tolls can be paid in cash or by QR code.

According to the project, when the uphill Nagdhunga road was congested, small vehicles used to take about 15 minutes and larger ones about 35 minutes. Through the tunnel, the trip from Sisnekhola in Dhading to Kisipidi in Balambu takes about seven minutes.

The main tunnel is 2.688 km long, with a 2.557 km emergency tunnel beside it. It is open 24 hours a day. Motorcycles, three-wheelers, pedestrians and non-motorized vehicles are not allowed, nor are vehicles carrying petrol, diesel, gas or explosives.

Japan's Hazama Ando Corporation built the tunnel and handed it over to the project in May 2026. The contractor is responsible for fixing any problems that appear during the first year after handover.
