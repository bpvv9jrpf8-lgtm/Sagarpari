Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang is back as the company's chief executive, 11 days after he resigned. On Monday, Nvidia's board voted unanimously to restore Huang to his roles as chairman, CEO and board member, effective immediately.

Huang stepped down on November 12 after it came to light that he had had two extramarital relationships. One of them was reportedly with a woman whom US intelligence agencies suspected of spying for China. Beijing has denied the allegation.

Board chair Harvey C. Jones said bringing Huang back was "in the best interests of shareholders, employees, partners and customers." According to the board, after detailed discussions with Huang it was satisfied with his clarity and leadership. After seeking the views of senior US government officials, the board concluded that his return poses no risk to national security.

The decision is a sharp reversal. Just a week earlier, the board had said it had no plans to bring Huang back, and it had described its acceptance of his resignation as unanimous.

Colette Kress, who had been serving as interim CEO, has returned to her role as chief financial officer. An investigation by the board's committee of independent directors into whether Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort is still ongoing. Huang has said he will cooperate fully with it.

Nvidia shares rose nearly 34% on the news of Huang's return. More than 150,000 people had signed an open letter urging the board to reinstate him. On November 17, Huang said he would be honored to return if the board wanted him. President Donald Trump also praised the board's decision as the right one.

Nvidia is one of the world's most valuable chip companies and dominates the market for artificial intelligence (AI) chips. Its shares had fallen nearly 33% after Huang resigned.
