The path to an employment-based green card may get narrower. On Monday, the U.S. Department of Labor released a proposal to add new limits and tougher checks to the permanent labor certification program, known as PERM, Reuters reported. It is the Trump administration's latest step to reduce legal immigration.

PERM is the first step for many employment-based green cards. Large tech companies and outsourcing firms use it to move foreign employees from temporary status to permanent residence. Before hiring a foreign worker, an employer must certify that there are not enough qualified and available U.S. workers for the job, and that hiring the foreign worker will not hurt the wages and working conditions of Americans doing similar work.

Under the proposal, employers would have to do more to show that hiring a foreign worker does not harm U.S. workers. That requirement would also be extended to occupations that have so far been exempt from individual labor market testing because of presumed shortages. The exemption list, called Schedule A, covers professional nurses and physical therapists as well as people of exceptional ability in the sciences or arts — and the proposal seeks to end Schedule A altogether, IANS reported. Because many Nepali nurses work in the U.S., the change could also affect their green card process.

There are new recruitment conditions too. The proposal would require the mandated recruitment activities to run for at least 90 calendar days and would have employers reach the widest pool of qualified U.S. workers through modern channels, such as electronic ads instead of print ads. It would also bar an employer from seeking PERM certification for a foreign worker within two years of laying off U.S. workers in a similar role. The department said it would also widen protections for displaced U.S. workers and the scope of notice about applications.

The proposal is not yet a final rule. It is set to be published in the Federal Register on Tuesday and will then be open for public comment for 60 days, so employers' obligations have not changed right away. Once a final rule is issued, the proposal calls for a 90-day transition period after its publication. Reuters noted that in October the U.S. government barred large tech companies including Microsoft and Adobe from using PERM — Microsoft is the program's biggest filer.

Nepali professionals moving from H-1B or other temporary visas toward a green card through PERM should follow the comment process and the status of the final rule closely.
