Nepal's government is moving to set up a commission to investigate cases linked to the Gen Z protests, compensate people who were affected and oversee reparations. An **ordinance giving the commission its legal basis reached the President's Office on Sunday, November 22.**

The Gen Z protests took place on **September 8 and 9, 2025**. The commission is meant to handle the cases that grew out of those events in a more systematic way.

## What the ordinance would do

According to the Office of the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers, the proposed commission would:

- provide a legal basis for further investigation of the events
- help bring about reconciliation between victims and other parties
- pay compensation for losses
- resolve cases other than serious crimes quickly and systematically

Nepal News reported that the ordinance is also meant to address the demands of people facing charges over the protests.

## Why an ordinance

An earlier Cabinet meeting decided to recommend that the President issue the ordinance. Under Nepal's constitution, the President can issue an ordinance on the government's recommendation when immediate action is needed and neither house of the Federal Parliament is in session. An ordinance carries the same legal force as an act of parliament.

Parliament is not in session now, which is why the government chose an ordinance instead of a bill.

## What we don't know yet

The commission's membership, its deadline and the amount or criteria for compensation have not been made public. Its full provisions will be known only after the President issues the ordinance.
