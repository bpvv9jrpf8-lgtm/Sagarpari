U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) has made it harder for young immigrants to get a green card through the Special Immigrant Juvenile (SIJ) program. Two policy alerts issued on November 20, 2026 (PA-2026-17 for the SIJ petition and PA-2026-18 for the green card stage) took effect immediately, including for cases already pending.

## Who the program is for

SIJ classification is for unmarried young people under 21 who were abused, neglected or abandoned by a parent and who have findings from a state juvenile court. In the past, the court order often carried most of the weight. Under the new guidance, USCIS will look at the factual basis behind the state court's findings itself.

A central question for officers is whether getting protection from parental mistreatment was a main reason for seeking the court's findings. To check this, USCIS may review government records, ask for more evidence and interview the young person.

## Green card stage

The new rules also cover the green card application (Form I-485). An approved SIJ petition does not guarantee a green card: approval still depends on USCIS's discretion. The new alert removes earlier guidance that counted simply meeting the basic eligibility requirements as a positive factor. USCIS may also send an individual biometrics notice (fingerprints and photo) in the green card process.

If the green card is denied, or the SIJ approval is revoked, USCIS may issue a Notice to Appear, the document that starts removal (deportation) proceedings in immigration court.

## Other changes

- USCIS removed the policy language that let young people book a field-office appointment to file Form I-360 in the final two weeks before turning 21. The petition must now be properly filed before the 21st birthday.
- USCIS made clear that an approved I-360 by itself does not give lawful status or permission to work.
- Cases approved before November 20 remain under the earlier policy.
