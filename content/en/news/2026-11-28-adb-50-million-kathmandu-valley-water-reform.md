The **Asian Development Bank (ADB)** has approved a **$50 million loan** (about Rs 7 billion) to reform water supply and sewerage services in Nepal's Kathmandu Valley, along with a $750,000 technical assistance grant, The Kathmandu Post and Nepal News reported Friday.

This is a **policy-based loan**, not a construction project. The money is tied to reforms in how the valley's water sector is organized, run and regulated, rather than to building new pipelines.

## What problems is it meant to fix?

According to the reports, the valley's water sector struggles with:

- Fragmented institutions that don't work together
- Weak financial and commercial performance by service providers
- Inadequate wastewater treatment
- A lack of coordinated planning and development

The program will help bring the sector's institutions under one stronger governance framework. It also backs the government's effort to turn the valley's water utility into a modern, financially sustainable and citizen-centered institution.

## Two phases

The program runs in two phases. According to the Post, the first phase has set up a unified governance structure and regulatory framework. The second phase will put those reforms into practice and make them permanent.

By **2030**, the program aims for:

- Measurable gains in operational, administrative and financial performance
- Better compliance with drinking-water quality standards
- Faster, more effective handling of customer complaints
- Wider access to reliable, high-quality, inclusive and resilient water and sewerage services

## What ADB said

Arnaud Cauchois, ADB's top official in Nepal, said reliable and sustainable water supply and sewerage services are "fundamental to public health." He said the program will complement ongoing investments in water infrastructure.

Founded in 1966, ADB has 69 members, 50 of them from the Asia-Pacific region.

## Why it matters to Nepalis in the US

For many Nepalis abroad whose families live in the Kathmandu Valley, water and sewerage are everyday concerns back home. Reforms like these take time to show results, but the 2030 targets give a sense of what service should look like. SagarPari will keep following the program's progress.
