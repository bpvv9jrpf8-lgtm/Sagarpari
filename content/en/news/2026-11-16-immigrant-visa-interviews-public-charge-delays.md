Immigrant visa applicants waiting to reunite with family in the United States still face uncertainty. Nearly three months after the U.S. State Department began canceling immigrant visa interviews worldwide to train consular officers on new public charge rules, no date has been announced for normal processing to resume, according to an update published Monday by Chodorow Law Offices.

Some embassies and consulates have restarted interviews, but not evenly. The department has confirmed that the new public charge procedures apply worldwide, not only to nationals of the 75 countries that earlier faced a visa pause. The new process may include a new public charge worksheet, extra review at the supervisor level, and requests for more documents or repeat interviews when needed. No firm timeline has been set for finishing the review of affected cases.

Applicants whose interviews were canceled should expect a notice with a new date from the embassy or consulate later. Some applicants who already had their interviews are also facing extra scrutiny and delays in visa issuance. Nonimmigrant visa appointments have not been canceled, and pre-interview processing through the National Visa Center has not stopped.

Meanwhile, the worldwide pause has been challenged in federal court. In Catholic Legal Immigration Network v. Rubio, a federal judge ruled on Aug. 21 that the visa pause on nationals of 75 countries was unlawful. On Nov. 6, the plaintiffs filed an amended complaint that also challenges the worldwide public charge directive. The court has not yet ruled on the new claims.

This month the department also launched a public charge bond pilot program, under which some applicants refused on public charge grounds may be issued a visa after posting a bond. Nepali applicants waiting on family-sponsored visas through the U.S. Embassy in Kathmandu should keep an eye on their case status and on embassy notices.
