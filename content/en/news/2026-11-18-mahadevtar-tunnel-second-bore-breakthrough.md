Workers on Nepal's Kathmandu–Tarai/Madhesh Expressway, often called the fast track, broke through the second bore of the Mahadevtar tunnel on Wednesday, Nov. 18. President Ramchandra Paudel inaugurated the breakthrough at Mahadevtar in Makawanpur district.

According to The Kathmandu Post, the bore that will carry traffic from Nijgadh toward Kathmandu is about 3.445 km long. The parallel bore, about 3.486 km, broke through in the third week of August. The tunnel links Bhimphedi and Bakaiya rural municipalities in Makawanpur.

## What officials said

Paudel said the expressway matters for Nepal's economic prosperity and national unity, and praised the Nepali Army, which is building it. Infrastructure Minister Sunil Lamsal and Chief of Army Staff Ashok Raj Sigdel also attended. Lamsal told the agencies involved to finish on schedule.

## Where the project stands

- **Length:** about 71 km (70.977 km), from Khokana in Lalitpur to Nijgadh in Bara, including about 11.2 km of tunnels.
- **Progress:** 50.54% physically complete, according to Nepal News.
- **Cost:** estimated at Rs 211.93 billion including VAT; Rs 94.22 billion had been spent by mid-September, the project says.
- **Bridges:** 89 planned; contracts awarded for 85, and 5 completed.

The Dhedre and Len Danda tunnels have also broken through and are in finishing work. Work continues on the Chandram Bhir, Sisautar, Devichaur and Mauribhir tunnels.

## Delays and land

The government handed the project to the Army on Aug. 11, 2017, with a completion target of December 2024. The deadline was pushed to mid-April 2027 and then to mid-April 2030. After nine years of construction, about half the work is done.

Land acquisition in the Khokana area remains the main hurdle. The Kathmandu Post reports that about 69,000 square meters of private land and more than 7,000 square meters of guthi (trust) land still need to be acquired, and decisions on the starting point and traffic management for the Khokana–Dukuchhap section are pending.

Once finished, the expressway will link the Kathmandu Valley directly with the Tarai–Madhesh plains. Paudel said he was confident the government and Army were committed to completing it on time.
