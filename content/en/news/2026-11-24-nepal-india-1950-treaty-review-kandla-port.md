Nepal and India have released a joint vision document saying their existing bilateral agreements, including the 1950 Treaty of Peace and Friendship, could be reviewed on the basis of mutual consent. The document was issued during Prime Minister Balendra Shah's November 22–23 state visit to India and has been posted by Nepal's Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

The document, reported to be in preparation before the visit, is titled "Viksit Bharat and Samunnat Nepal: Re-imagining the Partnership." According to the joint press release, both prime ministers adopted it.

## What it says about the treaty

Point 4 of the vision says the partnership should reflect today's political, economic and technological realities and rest on "an updated and forward-looking legal framework." In that context, it says existing agreements, including the 1950 treaty, "could be reviewed on the basis of mutual consent."

The same point says all outstanding matters, including water resources, energy, trade and commerce, and the boundary, are to be resolved through mutual discussion and dialogue. The joint press release adds that the two leaders directed their foreign secretaries to work on the outstanding boundary issues. The document does not set a timeline or a mechanism for reviewing the treaty.

## Ten pillars

The vision is built around 10 pillars of cooperation. The first covers a comprehensive economic and development partnership and the second covers physical and digital connectivity. The document says India will extend 15,000 crore Indian rupees (24,000 crore Nepali rupees) in development assistance: 3,500 crore Indian rupees in grants and an 11,500 crore Indian rupee line of credit for energy.

## Kandla port now, Paradip later

According to the joint press release, India announced a new route for Nepal's transit trade through Deendayal Port (Kandla) in Gujarat. Access to Paradip port in Odisha will be made available once the needed infrastructure is in place.

Onlinekhabar reported that Nepal currently uses the Kolkata–Haldia and Visakhapatnam ports for trade with third countries. Nepal had earlier sought Dhamra port in Odisha and Mundra port in Gujarat, but the final agreement named different ports in the same two states.

## Easier banking and phone services across the border

The press release says both sides agreed on the need to make it easier for citizens of one country living in the other to access services such as banking and telecom. That matters to the many Nepalis who live and work in India, but detailed procedures have not yet been announced.

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi accepted an invitation to visit Nepal. The release says dates will be set through diplomatic channels.
