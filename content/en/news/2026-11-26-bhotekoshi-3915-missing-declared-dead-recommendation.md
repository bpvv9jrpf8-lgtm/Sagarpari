Nepal's Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Executive Committee has recommended that the government formally declare **3,915 people** missing in the Rasuwa–Bhote Koshi flood dead. The decision came Thursday at the committee's 38th meeting, chaired by Home Minister Sudhan Gurung.

The final call now rests with the government. For many families, a formal declaration of death is what unlocks death registration, compensation, insurance claims and property matters, so this is a key step for relatives in Nepal and abroad.

## Who is on the list

Of the 3,915 people:

- **3,292 are Nepali citizens**, verified through records from 48 districts.
- **623 are foreign nationals**, verified through 19 diplomatic missions.

The reports do not give a country-by-country breakdown of the foreign nationals.

## The legal basis

The recommendation was made under Section 2(3) of the Ordinance to Amend Some Nepal Acts Related to Disasters, 2083 BS (2026), which allows the government to declare people missing in a disaster dead.

Before the recommendation, the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority issued two seven-day public notices inviting claims and objections to the lists. The list of Nepali citizens was built from local government recommendations and decisions of district disaster committees. Details of foreign nationals came from their diplomatic missions in Nepal or the missions responsible for Nepal.

India's embassy in Kathmandu has asked families of its 213 missing citizens to raise any objections by Dec. 2. Citizens of other countries, including the United States, are also among the missing, so relatives abroad can seek information through their own country's embassy or mission.

## Other decisions

The same meeting approved a national Early Warning for All roadmap (2083–2087 BS), a strategy and action plan for forest fire risk reduction and management, and an action plan for reducing air pollution-related disaster risks.

It also approved moving at-risk households to safer sites: 46 households in Alital Rural Municipality, Dadeldhura; 40 in Sandhikharka Municipality, Arghakhanchi; and 32 in Chumnubri Rural Municipality, Gorkha.

The Aug. 26 Bhote Koshi–Trishuli flood heavily damaged riverside settlements, roads, bridges and hydropower projects.
