More than 600 Nepali workers in Romania are facing uncertainty over their jobs and housing because of a dispute over wages and accommodation, The Kathmandu Post reported on Tuesday, November 24.

## What happened

According to the Post, the workers went to Romania through Times Management System Pvt Ltd, a recruitment agency based in Nepal. The workers say they each paid Rs 1 million (10 lakh Nepali rupees) to get the jobs. In Romania, they were placed with various companies through Bisar Logistics Pvt Ltd, a Romania-based firm.

The core of the dispute is pay. The contracts the workers signed in Nepal set a basic monthly salary of 2,699 Romanian lei (RON). Bisar Logistics has said it will pay only 2,455 lei a month — 244 lei less every month.

On top of that, the company has proposed deducting 300 lei a month for hostel accommodation. The workers say they were promised that housing would be free.

## Warning about the hostel

The workers made public a message from Bisar Logistics suggesting that anyone who did not report to work would have to leave the hostel. Talks with the agencies involved have not resolved the dispute. The Post's report did not include a response from the company.

## What the workers are asking for

The workers have urged Nepal's Ministry of Labour, Employment and Social Security and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to act immediately. They have also asked the Nepali embassy in Germany to contact both the company and the workers directly and carry out an impartial investigation.

Their demands include:

- An inquiry into the gap between the contracted salary and the salary now being offered
- A clear explanation of the 300-lei hostel deduction
- Assurance that the company will not fire them, evict them or pressure them

## A reminder for anyone planning to work abroad

Before leaving for a job overseas, it is worth checking that the contract clearly spells out salary, housing, food and other benefits, and keeping a certified copy with you. If problems come up, workers can file a written complaint with the relevant embassy and with Nepal's labour ministry.
