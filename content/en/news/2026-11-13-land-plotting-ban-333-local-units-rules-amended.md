Only **420** of Nepal's **753 local units** have finished classifying their land, so subdividing land into new plots is suspended in the remaining **333**. Because of the delays, the government is preparing to amend the **Land Use Regulations 2022** for the fourth time, The Kathmandu Post reported on Friday.

## What's happening

Under the law, each local unit must sort the land in its area into **10 categories**. Where that work is not done, land cannot be split up into new plots for sale.

According to The Kathmandu Post, the land management ministry has sent a draft amendment to the Finance Ministry. Ministry spokesperson **Ganesh Prasad Bhatta** said the draft will go to the Law Ministry after finance approval and take effect once the Cabinet approves it. Details of what the amendment will change have not been made public.

## What is still allowed

Even in units where plotting is halted, entire plots can still be sold as they are, inheritance transfers can go ahead, and court-ordered transactions are allowed. The ban mainly targets cutting land into new, smaller plots.

## Deadline after deadline

The 2022 regulations required agricultural land to be classified within six months. When local units missed that, a first amendment in 2023 added a year and relaxed minimum plot sizes. A second amendment followed in 2024, and a third pushed the agricultural deadline to mid-July 2026.

According to The Kathmandu Post:

- In September 2024, plotting was halted in **620** local units; only **133** had finished.
- From July 2025, it was suspended in **500** units, with **253** done.
- In August 2026, plotting stopped in **390** units.
- The number is now down to **333**.

Municipal Association of Nepal president **Bhim Prasad Dhungana** said classifying land costs each local unit at least **Rs 1.5 million to Rs 2 million**.

## What it means for Nepalis in the US

Many Nepalis in the US send money home to build a house or buy land. If you plan to buy a piece carved out of a larger plot, or to split family land among siblings, first check whether the local unit where the land sits has finished classification. Since subdivision is not possible where it hasn't, ask the land revenue office or municipality before paying any advance.
