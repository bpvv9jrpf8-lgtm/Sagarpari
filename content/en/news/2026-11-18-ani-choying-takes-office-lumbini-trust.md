Ani Choying Drolma, the Buddhist nun and singer known around the world as the "singing nun," formally took office Wednesday (Nov. 18) as **vice-chair of the Lumbini Development Trust**, about a month after Nepal's government appointed her.

## Prayers first, then the office

According to *The Kathmandu Post*, Drolma first offered prayers at the **Maya Devi Temple** after arriving in Lumbini, then went to the trust's office to take charge. *Ratopati* reported that trust staff welcomed her.

The trust's newly appointed **member-secretary, engineer Santosh Pandey**, also took office the same day. Ratopati said he is from Tilottama in Rupandehi. Trust information officer Dr. Haridhwoj Rai confirmed that both officials assumed their posts on Wednesday. The Post said the appointments complete the trust's executive committee.

## What she said

After taking office, Drolma said she would work for the protection and welfare of all living beings and promote Lumbini as a destination for **spiritual tourism**. She also pledged to work with all stakeholders to finish projects under the Lumbini master plan as soon as possible.

## Appointment and term

Nepal's Cabinet appointed Drolma on **Oct. 16**. Under Section 8 of the Lumbini Development Trust Act of 1985, she will serve a **five-year term**. By law, the tourism and culture minister is the trust's ex-officio chair, while the government appoints the vice-chair and other officials. The trust had gone without appointed officials for a long time.

## Background: the protests

Her appointment drew opposition in Lumbini. As SagarPari reported in October, local groups including the **Lumbini Bachau Mahaabhiyan** (Save Lumbini Campaign) held protests demanding that it be cancelled. *Nepal News* reported that she took office despite protest threats from the campaign. The sources do not say whether any protest took place on Wednesday.

Older questions raised by opponents — an investigation into money sent to her account from Hong Kong in 2018, and a complaint about her citizenship — remain allegations only; no authority has found her guilty of wrongdoing.

The Lumbini Development Trust manages the conservation and development of Lumbini, the birthplace of the Buddha and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, along with nearby sites such as Tilaurakot, Devdaha and Ramagrama.
