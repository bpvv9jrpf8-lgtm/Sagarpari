Shareholders in Nepal who can't afford to buy rights shares may soon be able to sell their right to them instead. The Securities Board of Nepal (Sebon) plans to introduce a policy within the current fiscal year that would let investors trade their rights entitlements on the Nepal Stock Exchange (Nepse).

## The problem today

A listed company can raise capital by offering new shares to its existing shareholders — a rights issue. Only investors who hold shares on a set date, known as the book-close date, are eligible.

Many of them don't have the money to buy the extra shares, or don't want to invest more. The law already allows entitlements to be transferred, but finding a buyer is hard, so in practice many rights simply go unused. Rights shares that aren't subscribed are later auctioned by the company.

## What would change

According to The Kathmandu Post, Sebon chair Gopal Bhatta said rights shares are an entitlement of existing shareholders, but investors can't benefit from them when they lack the money to invest.

Under the proposed system, investors could sell their entitlements through Nepse rather than privately or letting them lapse. Bhatta said entitlements should be traded separately so they don't affect the price of ordinary shares during regular trading. The board is considering letting entitlements trade on the exchange while a rights issue is open.

The Post notes that similar systems exist in the United Kingdom, Australia and India. In India, eligible shareholders can either buy the additional shares or trade their rights entitlements on an exchange during the rights period.

## What it means for major shareholders

Selling an entitlement instead of subscribing reduces a shareholder's percentage of ownership after the issue. The Post says that could weaken large shareholders' influence, including their ability to win board seats or shape company decisions.

Sebon has not yet announced a start date or the detailed trading rules. The change will take effect only after the board issues them.
