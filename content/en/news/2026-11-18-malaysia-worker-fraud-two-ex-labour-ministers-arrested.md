Nepal police have arrested **six people, including two former labour ministers**, in the investigation into illegal fees collected from Nepali workers heading to Malaysia for jobs. The Central Investigation Bureau (CIB) made the arrests on Wednesday at different locations in Kathmandu, Lalitpur and Sarlahi.

The case, which began with four arrests in mid-October, has now reached former ministers and senior civil servants.

## Who was arrested

According to the CIB's statement, those held are:

- **Sher Bahadur Kunwar**, former Minister for Labour, Employment and Social Security
- **Rameshwor Ray Yadav**, former Minister for Labour, Employment and Social Security
- **Binod KC**, former secretary at the labour ministry
- **Umesh Dhungana**, former joint secretary of the employment management division
- **Rajiv Pokharel**, former joint secretary of the employment management division (Ratopati reports he is currently director general of the Department of Transport Management)
- **Nirmala Gurung**, former law officer

## The allegations

The six are being investigated for **fraud and organized crime** under the Organized Crime Prevention Act, 2070 (2013). The CIB says money was illegally collected from workers in violation of the labour memorandum of understanding between the governments of Nepal and Malaysia.

Police allege the accused abused their positions to help move foreign currency out of the country illegally, failed to act against the exploitation of workers, and took part in decisions to send the money to Malaysia. The CIB puts the amount sent abroad at about **$8.37 million and £3.72 million — roughly Rs 1.69 billion**. It says this hurt the national economy and Nepal's foreign-exchange reserves.

The investigation began after a directive from the Office of the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers and a complaint filed by Bikash Ranabhat and others. The CIB obtained permission from the Kathmandu District Court for the arrests. The investigation is continuing.

These are allegations; no court has found anyone guilty.

## Background

The CIB's earlier findings said workers bound for Malaysia were charged an extra "biomedical service" fee on top of the government-set medical-test fee. Four people, including Kailash Khadka, were arrested in the case in October.

If you or a relative are preparing to work in Malaysia or elsewhere, keep receipts for every fee you pay, including for medical tests. Charges above the official rate can be reported to Nepal's Department of Foreign Employment or the police.
