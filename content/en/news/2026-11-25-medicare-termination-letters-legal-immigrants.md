The Social Security Administration (SSA) has started sending letters to nearly 300,000 legal immigrants on Medicare, telling them their coverage may end. According to The Washington Post, the people affected are older and disabled immigrants who have lived in the United States for years and paid into Medicare.

The letter says: "Our records show you may no longer be eligible for Medicare because of a change in the law." The new restriction comes from H.R. 1, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, and takes effect in 2027. Under that law, Medicare will be limited to U.S. citizens and nationals, lawful permanent residents (green card holders), Cuban and Haitian entrants, and residents of certain Pacific island nations covered by the Compacts of Free Association.

## Who is affected?

The affected groups include refugees, people granted asylum, Temporary Protected Status (TPS) holders and survivors of human trafficking who had qualified for Medicare through their work history. Patients with kidney failure who are on dialysis are also at risk, because Medicare has been paying for their dialysis.

This matters directly to the Nepali community in the U.S., because TPS holders are clearly among the affected groups. Nepali families on Medicare should check whether they have received a letter and act quickly if they have.

## What can you do?

For most of those affected, coverage will end on February 1, 2027. People who receive a letter can appeal within 30 days, either by calling 1-800-MEDICARE or by submitting documents showing they are still eligible. Many people who prove their eligibility with documents may be able to keep their coverage.

The final number is still uncertain. SSA is sending close to 300,000 initial notices, but estimates of how many people will actually lose coverage vary: the Congressional Budget Office estimates about 100,000, while the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services puts it at about 32,000.
