U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) arrested and deported more people in fiscal year 2026 than in any year in its history, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) said on Thursday. The federal fiscal year 2026 ran from October 1, 2025, to September 30, 2026.

## The numbers

According to DHS:

- ICE deported (removed) **483,200 people** in fiscal year 2026.
- ICE made **more than 491,000** immigration arrests in the same period. Nearly 475,000 of them were administrative arrests, which involve alleged civil immigration violations rather than criminal charges.
- In September 2026 alone, the agency reported more than 48,000 arrests and about 44,000 removals.

The previous record was 442,640 removals in fiscal year 2025. Newsweek notes there were 409,849 removals in fiscal year 2012 under President Obama and 271,484 in fiscal year 2024, the last full year of the Biden administration.

"Fiscal Year 2026 saw the highest number of ICE deportations and arrests for any year in ICE's history," DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said in the release. DHS also says more than 3 million migrants have left the United States since President Trump returned to the White House.

## Questions about comparisons

Newsweek reported that it had not independently verified the agency's figures. Aaron Reichlin-Melnick, a senior fellow at the American Immigration Council, said comparing removals across administrations could be misleading because Title 42 expulsions under President Biden are not counted in the removal totals.

The figures come days after President Trump said in a CBS "60 Minutes" interview that his administration's immigration enforcement may have been "too tough".
