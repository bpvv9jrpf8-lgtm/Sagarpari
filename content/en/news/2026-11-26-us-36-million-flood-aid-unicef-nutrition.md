The United States has committed **more than $36 million** to help Nepal respond to and recover from the devastating Bhote Koshi floods in late August, the U.S. Embassy in Kathmandu said Thursday (Nov. 26).

The embassy's acting chief, Chargé d'Affaires Megan Boldin, met Thursday with UNICEF's Asia-Pacific Regional Director Sanjay Wijesekera and UNICEF's Nepal Representative Alice Akunga. They discussed post-flood recovery priorities, coordination of humanitarian aid, and wider health and nutrition support.

## Where the money goes

According to the details the embassy released:

- **$2.5 million** to UNICEF from the State Department's disaster and humanitarian response funds, for emergency water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) and child protection services, with priority for children and other vulnerable groups.
- **$176,000** to restore vaccination services and vaccine storage in the hardest-hit districts.
- **$12 million** through UNICEF to improve maternal and child health and nutrition under the U.S. "America First" Global Health Strategy. The money supports breastfeeding promotion, infant feeding support, and the introduction of vitamin- and mineral-fortified supplementary food.

U.S. support has also made possible a national micronutrient survey, which will identify vitamin and mineral deficiencies and help guide Nepal's nutrition programs, the embassy said.

Boldin thanked UNICEF for mobilizing quickly after the floods and reaching affected communities, and praised its long partnership with Nepal on nutrition.

## A total first cited in October

The embassy first cited the "more than $36 million" figure on Oct. 16, when Boldin met House Speaker Dol Prasad Aryal. Thursday's release broke down part of that money going through UNICEF. The embassy did not give a full breakdown of the rest this time.

## Where recovery stands

On the same day, Nepal's disaster risk reduction and management executive committee recommended that the government declare 3,915 people still missing after the Aug. 26 floods dead. Three months on, [the Kathmandu Post reports](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/11/27/three-months-on-flood-survivors-still-await-government-response) that many survivor families are still waiting for resettlement and rent support as winter sets in.
