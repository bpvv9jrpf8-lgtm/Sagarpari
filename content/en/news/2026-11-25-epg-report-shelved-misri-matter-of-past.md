The report of the Nepal–India **Eminent Persons Group (EPG)**, set up to review the two countries' old treaties, now appears to have been quietly set aside. India's Foreign Secretary **Vikram Misri** has called it "a matter of the past."

Speaking to journalists on November 23, after Prime Minister Balendra Shah's two-day state visit to India (November 22–23), Misri was asked about the EPG. "I would say that is a matter of the past," he said, adding that India was "looking ahead on this particular issue, not looking backwards." Onlinekhabar English published a detailed report on the issue on Wednesday.

## What wasn't discussed

According to Misri, the EPG report, Lipulekh and India's Agnipath military recruitment scheme did not come up in talks between Shah and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi. On Lipulekh, he said India and China have long carried out trade through the pass. On Agnipath, he suggested contacting India's Ministry of Defence for policy questions.

Misri did not say outright that the new joint vision document, "Developed India and Prosperous Nepal: Reimagining the Partnership," replaces the EPG report, but he suggested it serves that purpose.

## Nepal: the vision document covers the EPG

Foreign Minister **Shishir Khanal**, speaking at a press conference at Tribhuvan International Airport, said the vision document was prepared in a way that also addresses the contents of the EPG report. The document, published by Nepal's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, says the two countries can review the 1950 Treaty of Peace and Friendship and other agreements by mutual consent.

EPG member **Nilambar Acharya** disagreed. "The Indian prime minister has simply refused to receive it. That does not mean the report has lost its relevance," he told Onlinekhabar.

## Background

The EPG was formed after Modi's 2014 visit to Nepal, when the two prime ministers agreed to review the 1950 treaty and other agreements. The group has completed its report. Under the agreed process it was to go first to the Indian prime minister and then to the Nepali prime minister, but it has never been submitted because India has not agreed to receive it.

Onlinekhabar also notes that the foreign secretary-level mechanism on the Kalapani and Susta border disputes has not met since 2014. The new joint statement repeats the instruction to the two foreign secretaries.
