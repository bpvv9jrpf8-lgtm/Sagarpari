Nepal's hopes of producing its own natural gas from Dailekh district are fading for now. More than a year after drilling ended at Jaljale in Bhairabi Rural Municipality, no further work has begun, and expensive equipment and site infrastructure are deteriorating, The Kathmandu Post reports.

## How much gas, and what is it worth?

Exploration overseen by the China Geological Survey estimated about **80.7 billion cubic meters** of gas at Jaljale. Ganesh Nath Tripathi, acting director-general of Nepal's Department of Mines and Geology, said the Chinese findings value the gas at nearly **$85 billion** and that it could meet Nepal's domestic demand for at least 50 years. He said drilling was completed at only one location; similar quantities are estimated at three other sites.

China's Xibu Drilling Engineering Company drilled for nine months, from mid-April 2024 to mid-January 2025. The Chinese side has shared its technical findings with the department but has not formally handed over the final report.

## What happens next?

Department Director-General Dinesh Kumar Napit said a discovery alone does not prove the gas can be extracted commercially. Nepal still needs **well testing** to measure how much gas the well can produce per hour, an analysis of the gas's composition, and a new agreement between the two governments. A department official said a letter was sent to the Chinese government through the Foreign Ministry about three months ago, with no reply so far.

Nepal and China reached an understanding on petroleum cooperation in 2016 and signed a formal agreement in 2019, when China pledged about Rs 2.5 billion in grant aid. Work originally meant to take three years was repeatedly delayed by Covid, trouble bringing in heavy equipment, poor roads and land acquisition problems.

## Locals left waiting

Dailekh's chief district officer, Ananda Saru, said about 70 technical staff and 100 local workers were employed during the work; the area is now largely deserted. Rita Kumari Shahi, chair of Bhairabi Rural Municipality, said the municipality set aside nearly Rs 20 million this year for infrastructure around the site and urged the government to move the project forward. Local community leader Dhan Bahadur Budha said land prices jumped from Rs 500,000 to as much as Rs 1.5 million per ropani after news of the find, but sales have since dried up.

## Why it matters

Department of Customs data show Nepal imports about Rs 28 billion worth of LPG a year through India and spends about Rs 6 billion annually just to transport it. Experts say domestic gas could cut imports and the trade deficit, but only after testing is done and a new government-to-government deal is in place.
