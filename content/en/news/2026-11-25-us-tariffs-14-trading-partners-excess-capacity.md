The Trump administration on Wednesday proposed new tariffs on 14 major trading partners, including China, India and Vietnam. The Office of the US Trade Representative (USTR) released the proposal after an eight-month investigation. The administration says these countries used industrial policy and subsidies to build far more factory capacity than the market needs, flooding world markets with cheap goods.

USTR had investigated 16 partners, including Japan, the European Union and South Korea, for unfair trade practices under Section 301. Tariffs are now proposed on China, Bangladesh, Cambodia, the EU, India, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand and Vietnam. No tariffs have been proposed for Mexico or Norway for now; the reviews of those two countries are still underway.

## Highest rate on China

Under the proposal, China would face the highest rate. The proposed extra 20% on Chinese goods would be added on top of the roughly 25% tariffs imposed during Trump's first term. But under the trade truce reached with Beijing a year ago, the overall rate on China would stay within a 20% limit. That truce expires on January 10.

Vietnam faces a proposed rate of up to 35% and India up to 25%. If they reach a reciprocal trade agreement with the US, Vietnam's rate could fall to 20% and India's to 17%.

Under existing trade deals, rates for the EU, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan would stay within a 15% cap. Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand face proposed rates of up to 18%, and Singapore up to 20%. About $30 billion worth of Chinese imports would be spared; the two countries are discussing exemptions for those goods through a "Board of Trade" process.

The proposed tariffs would not be added on top of the 10% or 12.5% tariffs imposed on 60 trading partners in July. Those rates are seen as an effort to rebuild the administration's global tariff system, which the Supreme Court struck down in February.

## India objects, China denies

India responded the same day, saying it has no structural excess capacity in any sector. New Delhi also said it remains committed to quickly concluding a trade deal with the US. China rejected the accusation, saying its exports reflect competitiveness.

US officials said the investigation was also partly aimed at Chinese investment in Southeast Asia. Washington alleges that goods made with Chinese investment in Vietnam, Indonesia and Cambodia are being shipped to the US market to get around earlier tariffs.

The proposed tariffs would take effect only after public notice, a comment period and hearings, which US officials said could push implementation into January. Written comments are due by January 5, 2027, and public hearings begin January 25.

## Impact on American consumers

The administration's tariffs have partly contributed to higher consumer prices in the US. In a Reuters/Ipsos poll taken after the midterm elections, 62% of respondents said they supported efforts to repeal tariffs.

The news also matters for Nepal: India and Bangladesh, both neighbors and major trading partners of Nepal, are on the list, and shifts like these in world trade can indirectly affect Nepali exporters and importers.
