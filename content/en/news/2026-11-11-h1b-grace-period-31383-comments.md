More than 31,000 public comments were filed before the deadline on a Department of Homeland Security (DHS) proposal to eliminate the 60-day grace period that H-1B and other work-visa holders get to find new options after their job ends. As of Tuesday night, 31,383 comments had been posted on the proposal.

## Comments surged at the end

The count climbed quickly in the final days. It stood at about 27,000 on November 2, reached 30,382 on Tuesday morning and passed 31,000 by that night. That is far more than two other pending DHS proposals tied to students and skilled workers drew: 8,947 comments on a plan to charge schools fees related to Optional Practical Training (OPT) for international students, and 686 on a plan to end deference to prior approvals, whose comment period runs until November 13.

## What the grace period does

DHS published the proposal on September 11. Under current rules, workers in categories such as H-1B, L-1, O-1 and TN, along with their dependents, may stay in the U.S. for up to 60 days after their employment ends, or until their authorized stay expires, whichever comes first. During that time they can look for another employer, change status or prepare to leave. If the proposal takes effect, that discretionary protection would disappear, and workers whose jobs end would generally have to leave the U.S. unless they have another basis to stay.

## DHS's reasoning

The department argues the grace period is not required by law and adds administrative work. According to DHS, officers had to consider whether the provision applied in more than 1.9 million petitions and applications between October 2017 and May 2026. Based on revoked employment petitions, the department estimates that an average of 65,752 foreign workers a year lost or changed jobs from fiscal years 2021 through 2025, and that an average of 3,795 of them had a new employer's petition filed within the grace period. DHS also acknowledges that if the rule takes effect, some workers could lose income, face relocation costs or end up in immigration proceedings.

## Who is most affected

Indian professionals would feel the biggest impact: according to USCIS's fiscal year 2025 report, about 70 percent of the 406,348 approved H-1B petitions were for people born in India, and about 12 percent for people born in China. For Nepali H-1B and other work-visa holders in the U.S., this window after a sudden job loss has often been critical for keeping a family in place and finding the next job.

It is important to be clear: the number of comments alone does not show how many people support or oppose the plan, and it is still only a proposal, not a rule in force. DHS will decide whether to publish a final rule only after reviewing all the comments, and any final rule would set its own effective date. The 60-day grace period was created by a 2016 rule and took effect on January 17, 2017.
