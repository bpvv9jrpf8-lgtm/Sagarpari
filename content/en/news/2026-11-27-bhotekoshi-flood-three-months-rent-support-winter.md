Thursday, November 26, marked three months since the August 26 Bhotekoshi flood. Bidur Municipality in Nuwakot has just started paying rent support to displaced families, but many survivors in Rasuwa and Nuwakot are still waiting for help. Winter has arrived, and the government has not yet decided where or how survivors will be permanently resettled.

## Who is getting rent support

At its September 11 meeting, Nepal's cabinet approved monthly rent support for displaced families for up to six months. A family of up to four receives **Rs 15,000** a month, plus Rs 2,000 for each additional member.

Radio Nepal reported that Bidur Municipality has begun depositing the money into the bank accounts of **970 families**. The municipality has identified **1,786 families** as eligible so far, a number that may still change. Verification is nearly complete in Belkotgadhi Municipality and in Kispang and Tarkeshwar rural municipalities, with payments expected afterward.

Bidur Mayor Rajan Shrestha told The Kathmandu Post that the municipality started paying one month's rent on November 20 and that about 1,000 families had received Rs 15,000. He said this November money came from donations by various organizations, not from the federal government. Radio Nepal, however, reported that the payments are being made under the federal program to avoid duplicate payments.

## Why many are left out

According to the Post, applicants must declare that they own no habitable house anywhere in Nepal. A survivor from Sole in Nuwakot said that clause has kept her family from receiving rent support. Families who take rent support also cannot claim temporary-shelter aid, forcing survivors to choose between the two.

Mayor Shrestha said a home cannot be built within Bidur's Rs 450,000 ceiling, and that nongovernmental organizations need federal approval before they can build houses.

## Harder still in Rasuwa

The Post reported that three-month rental agreements for about 80 to 90 families in Rasuwa have already expired. Buchung Tamang, chair of Amachhodingmo Rural Municipality, said the Syaphrubesi bridge has not been rebuilt and the area remains cut off from the road network. Aid organizations have also begun to pull out.

## What comes next

On Thursday, Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority recommended one-time rebuilding grants of Rs 600,000 to Rs 800,000, depending on the region, for families who lost their homes. The cabinet has yet to decide.

If relatives in Nepal were affected, encourage them to follow notices from their municipality or rural municipality and the District Administration Office. Once a local government verifies a family, the money is deposited directly into their bank account.
