Nepal's outstanding public debt stood at **Rs 2.975 trillion** at the end of Kartik (mid-November), the first four months of fiscal year 2026/27. That is equal to **45.08% of the country's GDP**.

The figures come from a Public Debt Management Office report, as reported by Onlinekhabar English on Wednesday. Total debt was Rs 2.981 trillion at the start of the fiscal year, so it has dipped slightly. The report attributes the decline mainly to exchange-rate gains — in other words, shifts in the rupee's value against foreign currencies lowered the rupee value of Nepal's foreign loans, even as the government kept borrowing.

## More than half is foreign debt

- **External debt:** Rs 1.574 trillion, or 52.93% of the total
- **Domestic debt:** Rs 1.400 trillion, or 47.07%

External debt comes from foreign governments and international lenders. Domestic debt is raised inside Nepal, mainly by selling government bonds.

## Borrowing well behind target

The government plans to borrow **Rs 658.28 billion** this fiscal year — Rs 410 billion domestically and Rs 248.28 billion from abroad.

By the end of Kartik it had raised only **Rs 153.84 billion**, or 23.37% of the annual target:

- Domestic: Rs 125 billion (30.49% of the domestic target)
- External: Rs 28.84 billion (11.62% of the external target)

That leaves **Rs 504.43 billion** still to raise in the remaining eight months — Rs 285 billion at home and Rs 219.43 billion from abroad. External borrowing is lagging furthest behind.

## Why it matters

As debt grows, a larger share of each year's budget goes to principal and interest payments, which can leave less for development projects. Repaying foreign loans also requires foreign currency, and remittances sent home by Nepalis working abroad are one of the country's biggest sources of it. That makes the numbers relevant for the Nepali diaspora in the US, too.

The Public Debt Management Office publishes debt figures every month.
