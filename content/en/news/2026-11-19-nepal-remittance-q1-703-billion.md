Money sent home by Nepalis working abroad is off to a strong start this fiscal year. According to Himal Press, the latest macroeconomic and financial situation report from Nepal Rastra Bank, the central bank, shows that Nepal received **Rs 703.7 billion** in remittances in the first three months of fiscal year 2026/27 (mid-July to mid-October).

That is **27.2% more** than in the same period a year earlier. Growth has slowed somewhat, though: a year ago, first-quarter remittances rose 35.4%.

## $4.6 billion in dollar terms

Measured in US dollars, remittances reached **$4.6 billion**, up 16.7%, compared with 29.2% growth a year earlier. The gap between the rupee and dollar growth rates reflects exchange-rate changes.

The month of **Asoj (mid-September to mid-October) alone brought Rs 267.34 billion**, up from Rs 201.22 billion in the same month last year.

## Fewer first-time migrant workers

The report shows **121,879 people** received first-time labor approvals for foreign employment in the three months, down from 123,459 a year earlier.

Re-entry approvals, for workers returning to jobs abroad, rose to 82,369 from 77,257.

## Foreign reserves top $27 billion

Strong inflows have also shored up Nepal's external position:

- **Gross foreign exchange reserves** reached Rs 4,161 billion in mid-October, up 6.8% from mid-July. In dollar terms that is **$27.39 billion**.
- The reserves are enough to cover an estimated **19.9 months of merchandise imports**, or 17.2 months of goods and services imports.
- The **current account** posted a surplus of Rs 279.11 billion.
- The **balance of payments** showed a surplus of Rs 303.79 billion.

## Spending on study abroad fell

Of interest to Nepali students and families paying for study in the US and elsewhere: education-related travel spending fell 14.7% in the three months to **Rs 36.65 billion**, from Rs 42.96 billion a year earlier.

Total travel payments dropped 15.7% to Rs 54.46 billion, while travel income rose 32.7% to Rs 25.52 billion.

## Why it matters

Money sent by hundreds of thousands of Nepalis working in the US, the Gulf, Malaysia and elsewhere is a pillar of Nepal's economy. Higher remittances strengthen foreign reserves and make it easier to pay for imports. The drop in first-time labor approvals, however, is a shift in overseas employment worth watching.
