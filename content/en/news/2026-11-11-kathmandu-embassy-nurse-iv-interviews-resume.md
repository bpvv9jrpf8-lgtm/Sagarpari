There is good news for Nepali nurses waiting to move to the United States. The US Embassy in Kathmandu has started scheduling employment-based immigrant visa interviews again, according to a new embassy update from the immigration law firm Musillo Unkenholt (MU).

MU's client data shows Nepal is one of the largest source countries for international nurse green card cases. The embassy's interview schedule had been disrupted by the immigrant visa pause on 75 countries and a worldwide pause on public charge training.

According to MU, new appointments are now coming for employment-based cases that had already been interviewed earlier this year but could not receive visas while the pause was in place. In other words, applicants who were interviewed but not issued visas are seeing their cases move again.

Under the State Department's immigrant visa scheduling tool, Kathmandu is now interviewing cases that have a current priority date and became documentarily complete in January 2026. MU notes this backlog is shorter than at many other embassies.

MU also warns that the backlog could grow, as the embassy prioritizes rescheduling interviews from early 2026, when visa issuance had almost completely stopped.

Applicants called for interviews should also pay attention to documents. As at other US embassies, Kathmandu's appointment emails ask applicants to bring public charge documents: details of their US job, assets and liabilities, health insurance and childcare plans. It is wise to have these ready before the interview.

MU says it will keep monitoring the Kathmandu embassy's interview schedule and document requirements.
