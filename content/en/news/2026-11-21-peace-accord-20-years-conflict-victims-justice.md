Today, November 21, marks **20 years since Nepal signed the Comprehensive Peace Agreement (CPA)** that ended its armed conflict. But thousands of victims and survivors are still waiting for justice, reparations and answers about relatives who disappeared, according to the **International Commission of Jurists (ICJ)**, a global human rights group.

The conflict ran from 1996 to 2006 and formally ended with the agreement signed on November 21, 2006. The ICJ issued its statement ahead of the 20th anniversary, The Kathmandu Post reported.

## "The burden of the State's failure"

**Mandira Sharma**, the ICJ's regional director for its Asia and Pacific Programme, said conflict victims "continue to bear the burden of the State's failure to deliver justice." She said victims have shown patience and a willingness to engage but have yet to see concrete results, and she urged the current government to honor its commitments.

According to the ICJ, enforced disappearances, unlawful killings, torture, ill-treatment and sexual violence from the conflict era remain unaccounted for.

## A long, stop-start process

The Post laid out how Nepal's transitional justice process has moved forward and stalled:

- **2014:** Parliament passed the act creating the commissions on disappeared persons and on truth and reconciliation.
- **February 2015:** The Supreme Court struck down several provisions and ordered amendments, including ruling out amnesty for serious violations.
- **2024:** Parliament amended the law. The ICJ calls this progress but says significant gaps remain.
- **May 2026:** The new government cancelled the process for appointing commissioners.

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission (TRC) and the Commission of Investigation on Enforced Disappeared Persons (CIEDP) have been set up and reconstituted several times. Despite receiving tens of thousands of complaints, neither has delivered meaningful accountability, truth-seeking or reparations, the report said. The UN human rights office (OHCHR) has also raised concerns.

## What the ICJ is asking for

The ICJ urged the government to:

- Promptly set up the TRC and CIEDP through independent, merit-based appointments
- Bring the law fully in line with Nepal's international obligations
- Ensure investigations and, where warranted, prosecutions
- Guarantee victims' participation at every stage
- Provide comprehensive reparations
