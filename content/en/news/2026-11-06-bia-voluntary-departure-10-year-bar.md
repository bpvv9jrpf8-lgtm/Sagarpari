People who are granted voluntary departure and do not leave the United States by their deadline cannot escape the resulting 10-year bar on relief by filing a motion to reopen afterward, the Board of Immigration Appeals (BIA) ruled on November 5. The precedent decision, Matter of Ontiveros-Diaz, 30 I&N Dec. 222 (BIA 2026), is binding on all immigration courts.

Voluntary departure lets a person leave the country at their own expense by a set date without a formal removal order on their record. Under INA section 240B(d)(1), someone who fails to leave in time can be fined between $1,000 and $5,000 and is barred for 10 years from relief including cancellation of removal, adjustment of status (a green card), change of status and registry.

## The case

Daniel Ontiveros-Diaz had sought cancellation of removal, arguing that his three U.S. citizen children would face exceptional hardship. An immigration judge denied that application but granted voluntary departure. On April 4, 2025, the Board dismissed his appeal and reinstated a 60-day voluntary departure period, with a final deadline of June 3, 2025. He did not leave. Fourteen days later, on June 17, he filed a motion to reopen to submit new medical and educational evidence about the children.

The Board denied the motion for two reasons. First, the 10-year bar that follows a broken voluntary departure agreement applies even when someone tries to add new evidence to an application they had already filed — "further relief" under the law includes that earlier application. Second, the new evidence mostly expanded on evidence already in the record, so the Board did not see it as likely to change the outcome, especially since the children would remain in the United States with their mother.

## The deadline trap

The most practical lesson is about timing. A voluntary departure period is at most 60 days, while the usual deadline for a motion to reopen is 90 days. Under 8 C.F.R. section 1240.26(e)(1), a motion filed before the voluntary departure period ends automatically terminates the grant, and the 10-year bar does not apply. But under section 1240.26(e)(2), a motion filed after the period ends has no effect on the bar. In these cases, the real deadline is not 90 days — it is the day voluntary departure expires. The rule that moves a deadline falling on a weekend or holiday to the next business day still applies.

There are some exceptions. For VAWA applicants who are victims of domestic violence, the bar may not apply if the abuse was the main reason for the delay. A failure to leave may also not count as "voluntary" if, through no fault of their own, the person did not know about the order or was physically unable to leave. But these exceptions are not automatic — they must be claimed with evidence.

The message for anyone with a grant of voluntary departure, Nepalis included, is clear: mark the final date in the decision on your calendar, file any motion before that date, and if you do leave, keep proof of your departure.
