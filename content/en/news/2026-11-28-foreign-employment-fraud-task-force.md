Nepal's government is preparing a **special task force** of several agencies to curb fraud tied to foreign employment and to consultancies that send students abroad, OnlineKhabar English reported Saturday. The Ministry of Youth, Labour and Employment is leading the effort.

## What was decided

A high-level meeting chaired by Home Minister **Sudan Gurung** on Nov. 20 decided to set up the task force. The same meeting also decided to create separate targeted teams within the Ministry of Education and Sports, Nepal Police, the Department of Foreign Employment (DoFE) and the Department of Immigration.

Each team will share information within its own area. Foreign employment fraud cases will go to DoFE, while human trafficking information will go to Nepal Police. Team heads will meet every week under a division chief at the labour ministry to review progress and plan strategy.

Home Ministry spokesperson Anand Kafle said targeted teams already exist at the Immigration Department, DoFE and the police. The goal, he said, is better coordination and closer monitoring to identify people involved in illegal activity.

## The scale of the problem

More than **35,000** fraud cases are under investigation at DoFE alone, and about **5,000** new complaints come in each year, according to the report.

Several complaint channels already exist. The Foreign Employment Act, 2007 gives DoFE powers to monitor recruitment agencies, conduct raids and investigations, issue arrest warrants and make arrests. Complaints can also be filed through the labour ministry's Shramadhan call centre, the Prime Minister's Office "Hello Sarkar" platform, and the ministry's Action Command Centre (ACC) under Labour Minister **Ramji Yadav**, which accepts complaints by WhatsApp, Viber, email, phone or in person.

## Earlier efforts faded

After the Gen Z movement, the previous government formed a task force with members from DoFE, the Office of the Attorney General and Home Ministry security agencies. Former labour minister Rajendra Singh Bhandari also set up a roughly 15-member mechanism through a secretary-level decision, but it stopped working effectively after the government changed.

Migration expert Rameshwar Nepal said a body created only by executive decision lacks legal authority. Instead of adding new structures, he said, the government should strengthen DoFE itself with more investigators and training.

## Why it matters for Nepalis abroad

Many Nepalis in the US have relatives back home who plan to go abroad through recruitment agencies or consultancies. Before paying any agency, families can check whether it is registered with DoFE, and report fraud through the official channels above.
