After more than two years of waiting and a weeks-long trial, a verdict came on Tuesday in Prince William County, Virginia, in the **Mamta Kafle Bhatt** case that shook the Nepali community. A jury found **Naresh Bhatt**, 39, guilty on all three counts, including the murder of his wife Mamta. The verdict gives Mamta's family a first major answer in their long search for justice; the 28-year-old nurse disappeared in July 2024.

On Tuesday the prosecution called its final witnesses. Toxicologist Dr. Abigail Kearns testified that the pesticide aluminum phosphide can produce phosphine gas inside the body, which can cause serious illness and death. Naresh's former roommate Akash Raccha testified that in 2021 Naresh threatened him and his wife with a gun. The prosecution then rested. After closing arguments, the jury began deliberating in the afternoon and returned its verdict in the evening.

The jury convicted Naresh of **first-degree murder**, the most serious charge, as well as concealing a dead body and related evidence tampering, and of improper treatment of a dead body. According to 7News, prosecutors presented about 100 witnesses and nearly 500 pieces of evidence and exhibits.

What makes the case unusual is that Mamta's body has never been found. Prosecutors built their case on scientific and circumstantial evidence, the investigation and witness testimony. During the trial, Naresh took the stand and admitted that he found Mamta unconscious and then dismembered her body and disposed of it in various places, but denied killing her. Testimony from Mamta's mother and brother that she was not suicidal, along with the toxicologist's evidence, appears to have led the jury to accept the prosecution's case.

Tuesday's verdict decides guilt only. Sentencing will follow a hearing on **March 12, 2027**. CNN reports that first-degree murder in Virginia carries 20 years to life in prison, and each of the two body-related counts carries up to five more years. The court denied Naresh bail until sentencing.

Members of the Nepali community and Mamta's family who gathered outside the court were emotional after the verdict. Mamta's mother, Gita, said the family had received justice after 832 days of waiting and thanked the community for its support. Mamta was last seen on July 27, 2024, at UVA Health Prince William Medical Center in Manassas, where she worked as a nurse.
