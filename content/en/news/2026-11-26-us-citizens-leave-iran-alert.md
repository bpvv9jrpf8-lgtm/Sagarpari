The United States has told its citizens in Iran to leave the country immediately. In a security alert issued Wednesday, the US Virtual Embassy in Iran said Americans should not travel to Iran for any reason and that those already there should depart right away. The alert also warned that conflict across the Middle East could escalate quickly.

The warning follows a string of recent attacks. Last week Iran struck US facilities in Bahrain and Kuwait. According to Bahrain's Interior Ministry, Saturday's attack injured two people and set fire to a civilian industrial site. Iran-backed Houthi rebels have also attacked targets in Saudi Arabia, including the capital, Riyadh.

The embassy said Iran, the Houthi movement and other groups close to Tehran could target US citizens, companies and institutions not only inside Iran but across the region. Americans in the Middle East were told to stay highly alert, keep an eye on flights and airspace conditions, and make backup plans in advance, since flights can be cancelled or airspace closed without notice.

The alert also advised Americans to avoid protests and crowds, stay away from areas with a heavy police presence, and be careful at public places linked to the United States. US citizens can sign up for the State Department's Smart Traveler Enrollment Program to receive security updates. The US has had no embassy in Iran since 1980; the US Interests Section at the Swiss Embassy in Tehran assists American citizens.

This is the second such alert this month — a similar warning was issued on Nov. 7. Separately, Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy claimed Wednesday morning that two oil tankers had struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz, but that claim has not been independently confirmed. The strait is a key route for the world's oil supply, so tension there can also affect global markets.
