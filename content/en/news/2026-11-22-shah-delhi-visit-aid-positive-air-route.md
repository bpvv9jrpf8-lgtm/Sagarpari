Prime Minister Balendra Shah travels to New Delhi on Sunday, Nov. 22, for a two-day state visit to India. Ahead of his arrival, India has responded positively to Nepal's request for about $2 billion in assistance, The Kathmandu Post reports. But the two sides have not yet agreed on a new air entry route for Nepal through Indian airspace.

## Aid: a positive signal, not a done deal

Nepal is seeking roughly $2 billion in grants and lines of credit from India. According to the Post, New Delhi has reacted favorably to the proposal. It has not been formally approved, and the amount and form of any assistance are expected to become clear during the visit.

## What is holding up the air route

Officials say negotiations on the air route are still going on. The main sticking points:

- **Military aircraft:** India has proposed that its military aircraft also be allowed to use the Bagdogra entry point. "We have made it clear that we can allow civilian flights, not military ones," a Nepali official told the Post.
- **Altitude:** India has proposed 39,000 feet for planes entering Nepal through Indian airspace. Nepal wants about 29,000 feet.
- **Western route:** Nepal wants the Kathmandu–Mahendranagar route (L626) to work in both directions. Right now, international flights can use it to leave Nepal but not to enter.

## The schedule

Ambika Joshi, spokesperson for the Nepali Embassy in New Delhi, said the prime minister lands in Delhi Sunday afternoon. Nepali officials said he will be received at Indira Gandhi International Airport by India's Minister of State for Commerce and Industry, Jitin Prasada. External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar is scheduled to pay a courtesy call at his hotel the same day.

On Monday, after delegation-level talks with Prime Minister Narendra Modi, several agreements are expected to be signed, some projects inaugurated, and a direct Janakpur–Ayodhya rail service unveiled. Nepal's foreign ministry said memorandums of understanding on education, health, agriculture, sports and disaster management will be exchanged. A joint vision on development and prosperity is planned to be released with a joint communiqué at the end of the visit, though the wording is still under discussion.

## A short visit that breaks with tradition

Onlinekhabar reports the trip will last only about 25 hours, with no stops outside Delhi. There was no traditional guard of honour or send-off at Kathmandu's airport. Shah is not expected to meet business leaders himself or visit the Nepali Embassy; Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle and Foreign Minister Shishir Khanal will handle business meetings.

The Post reported Saturday that no Indian minister is expected at an India–Nepal business roundtable in Delhi on Sunday. According to Onlinekhabar, India invited Shah soon after he took office, and the visit took about eight months to arrange.
