Private carrier Buddha Air has revived its long-shelved plan to launch international jet flights from Pokhara International Airport. According to The Kathmandu Post, the airline is running a technical assessment of aircraft for international service and studying whether Pokhara could become a regional aviation hub.

Buddha Air chairman Birendra Bahadur Basnet shared the plan on Tuesday at an interaction in Kathmandu organized by the Institute for Integrated Development Studies (IIDS), titled "Operating from Pokhara International Airport." Once the technical evaluation is done, he said, the company will study potential markets.

## Three jets, run by a separate company

The plan calls for three jets at first. Buddha Air would not fly them itself; it is considering a separate company, possibly set up as a public-private partnership or through an initial public offering (IPO). Ownership and financing have not been finalized.

Basnet said representatives of Airbus and Boeing are expected to visit Nepal to assess whether their aircraft can operate from Pokhara. "We need at least 1.5 million passengers annually to make the operation financially feasible from Pokhara," he said.

Buddha Air first announced international flights from Pokhara with Airbus A320 jets back in 2017, to start once the airport was finished. That never happened because of construction delays and incomplete infrastructure.

## An airport without flights

Pokhara International Airport cost $216 million. It was supposed to be finished by July 2021 but opened only on January 1, 2023. Today its only regular international service is flydubai. Industry stakeholders estimate the airport needs at least 30 landings and takeoffs a day to be financially viable.

Gautam Buddha International Airport in Bhairahawa faces the same problem. Built for more than Rs 35 billion and inaugurated on May 16, 2022, it currently has no regular international flights.

## New navigation system within a month

The Civil Aviation Authority of Nepal (CAAN) is preparing to install RNP-AR, a satellite-based navigation procedure, at Pokhara. It lets aircraft fly precise paths, including curved approaches, into airports surrounded by terrain. The visibility needed to land would drop from the current 2,800 meters to about 1,500 meters. CAAN spokesperson Bikram Gautam said installation would be completed within a month and would sharply cut weather-related delays and cancellations.

Speakers at the event stressed that better navigation alone will not make the airport viable; airlines still need passengers, competitive costs and markets. Mukesh Dahal, a deputy director at CAAN, said the focus should be on getting both international airports running and building demand rather than arguing over who built them.

## What it means for Nepalis abroad

For Nepalis in the US and elsewhere, direct international flights to Pokhara or Bhairahawa could one day mean skipping the connection through Kathmandu. But Buddha Air's plan is still at an early stage. No date, destinations or aircraft type for a first flight have been announced.
