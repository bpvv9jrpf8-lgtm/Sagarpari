Seven months after Prime Minister Balendra Shah's government took office promising an investment-friendly climate, Nepal's private sector says its confidence has not come back. Business leaders voiced the complaint on Friday in Biratnagar at a session titled "How to Boost Business Confidence?" during Kantipur Media Group's Ujyalo Purba dialogue, The Kathmandu Post reported.

## What's the problem?

Business leaders said the main issue is not interest rates but unpredictable government decisions, layers of administration and fear of investigation or prosecution. Bidur Kumar Lingthep, Koshi Province's minister for economic affairs and planning, acknowledged that worries about arrests and searches of business premises have continued since the elections.

Prakash Mundada, former president of the Morang Chamber of Industries and Commerce, said confidence remains low even though the government has a near two-thirds majority in the House. He said businesspeople spend 80% of their time on management matters rather than on their businesses.

Bholeshwar Dulal, senior vice-president of the Chamber of Industries, Morang, said factories in the region run at less than 40% of capacity. The area once had more than 600 factories employing over 100,000 people, he said. An initial environmental examination can take up to three years, and a three-kilometre access road to industrial areas still hasn't been built. "We don't need lower interest rates; we need stable policies," Dulal said.

Chandra Dhakal, former president of the Federation of Nepalese Chambers of Commerce and Industry, said many amended laws have not been implemented and urged the government to monitor them at the local level.

## Export potential

Mahesh Kumar Jaju, managing director of Asian Thai Foods, said his company exports to 29 to 30 countries. He said Koshi Province accounts for 20% of national production and 37% of exports, with tea exports of about Rs4 billion and chhurpi exports of about Rs5 billion a year.

## The government's response

Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle repealed 15 outdated laws on fiscal, administrative and property matters soon after taking office on March 27. The ruling Rastriya Swatantra Party's 100-point roadmap aims to double the economy to $100 billion by 2031 with 7% annual growth.

Wagle said he inherited a decade of stagnation, weak private investment and challenges such as the August 26 Bhotekoshi floods. He said interim economic measures would come within one to two weeks and that a capital market reform action plan is ready.

For Nepalis in the U.S. weighing investments back home, those interim measures will be worth watching.
