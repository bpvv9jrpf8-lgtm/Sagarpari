Nepal's government plans to give **every Nepal Premier League (NPL) franchise its own floodlit cricket stadium**. Prime Minister **Balendra Shah** announced the plan on Saturday in a social media post congratulating Sudurpaschim Royals on winning the league's third season.

Once the grounds are ready, the NPL could move to a **home-and-away format starting with its sixth edition**, Shah said. Right now, every NPL match is played in Kathmandu.

## Which team gets which ground?

According to The Kathmandu Post and Ratopati, the list shared by the prime minister is:

- **Biratnagar Kings** — Baijanathpur Cricket Stadium
- **Lumbini Lions** — Siddhartha Cricket Stadium
- **Karnali Yaks** — Surkhet Cricket Stadium
- **Sudurpaschim Royals** — Phapla Cricket Stadium
- **Kathmandu Gorkhas** — Kirtipur Cricket Ground
- **Pokhara Avengers** — Pokhara Cricket Stadium
- **Janakpur Bolts** — Ram Janaki Cricket Stadium
- **Chitwan Rhinos** — Gautam Buddha Cricket Stadium

## When will they be built?

The stadiums will be built through the **Ministry of Infrastructure Development**, according to Shah. Work on the Phapla Cricket Stadium has already moved forward, and work on the other seven grounds is **expected to begin within the next three months**. Shah said the government is committed to building the infrastructure needed to develop and expand professional cricket.

At the NPL's season opener on October 26, Infrastructure Development Minister Sunil Lamsal had announced floodlit stadiums seating at least 8,000 people in eight cities within three years, without naming the cities. The prime minister has now matched a ground to each team and named the edition when home-and-away play would begin. No budget has been made public.

## Why it matters

Tickets for this year's NPL final sold out within seconds, and there have been calls for the league to stop playing every match in Kathmandu and take the game to fans across the country. A home-and-away format would let fans in the provinces watch their teams play in their own cities.

In Saturday's final in Kirtipur, Sudurpaschim Royals beat defending champions Lumbini Lions by 10 wickets to win their first NPL title.

For Nepali cricket fans in the United States, it also means future NPL broadcasts could come from stadiums across the country, not just the capital.
