Nepal Sambat, Nepal's own traditional calendar, enters its new year 1147 today, Tuesday. Cultural programs are being held across the country, and the Newa community is also observing Mha Puja today, the traditional ritual of worshipping one's own self and soul.

Nepal Sambat is believed to date back to the reign of the Lichchhavi-era king Raghavdev. According to historical accounts, Shankhadhar Sakhwa, now honored as a national hero, started the era in 880 AD (937 Bikram Sambat) after paying off the debts of poor citizens. That is why the new year is also tied to the memory of debt relief and service to society.

The calendar enjoyed national recognition until the time of Prithvi Narayan Shah. After democracy arrived in 2007 BS, efforts began to restore its official status. Former Prime Minister Krishna Prasad Bhattarai declared Sakhwa a national hero in 2056 BS, and former Prime Minister Pushpa Kamal Dahal announced national recognition for Nepal Sambat in 2008 (2065 BS).

The new year is celebrated with special enthusiasm in places with Newa settlements, including Kathmandu, Lalitpur, Bhaktapur, Banepa, Dhulikhel, Barhabise and Bhimeshwar in Dolakha. The tradition of closing old account books and opening new ones also falls on this day, Kartik Shukla Pratipada.

On the meaning of Mha Puja, ritual expert Prof. Dr. Ramchandra Gautam says human consciousness and the soul are regarded as supreme. People believe that worshipping one's inner self pleases the gods, brings blessings and gives meaning to human life. Because the Pratipada lunar day falls across two days this year, some communities celebrated the new year on Monday, while others are performing Mha Puja today.

Today is also the fourth day of Tihar, when Govardhan Puja and Goru Puja (worship of oxen) are observed alongside Mha Puja. Tihar ends tomorrow, Wednesday, with Bhai Tika, and this year's auspicious time for Bhai Tika has been set at 11:07 a.m.
