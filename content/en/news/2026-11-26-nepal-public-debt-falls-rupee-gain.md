Nepal's public debt, which crossed Rs 30 trillion a few weeks ago, has eased slightly. According to the Public Debt Management Office, the government's outstanding debt stood at **Rs 29.75 trillion** at the end of Kartik (Nov. 16), down from Rs 29.81 trillion at the end of the last fiscal year in mid-July.

The main reason is not heavy repayment but a **weaker U.S. dollar**. Nepal Rastra Bank data show one dollar cost about Rs 153 at the end of the last fiscal year and about Rs 148 now, so dollar-denominated foreign loans look smaller in rupee terms. The debt office puts the exchange-rate gain at Rs 43.11 billion.

## Foreign debt down, domestic debt up

- **External debt:** Rs 15.74 trillion (Rs 16.06 trillion in mid-July)
- **Domestic debt:** Rs 14.01 trillion (about Rs 13.75 trillion in mid-July)

Total debt equals **45.08% of GDP** — 21.22% domestic and 23.86% external.

## Borrowing well behind target

The government plans to borrow Rs 658.28 billion in fiscal year 2026/27. In the first four months it raised Rs 153.84 billion, or 23.37% of the target:

- Domestic: Rs 125 billion of a Rs 410 billion target
- External: Rs 28.84 billion of a Rs 248 billion target

## Rs 140 billion on debt service

In the same four months the government spent **Rs 140.93 billion** on principal and interest payments — 33.73% of the Rs 417.88 billion set aside for debt service this year.

As noted in the OnlineKhabar report, the High-Level Economic Reform Recommendation Commission has warned that rising debt payments could squeeze spending on education, health and security. The National Natural Resources and Fiscal Commission recommends keeping federal domestic borrowing within 5.5% of GDP and not borrowing domestically to pay for recurrent and administrative costs.

## Why it matters to Nepalis abroad

Foreign loans must be repaid in dollars, and remittances are one of Nepal's main sources of foreign currency. A stronger rupee lightens the government's external debt load, but it also means families back home get slightly fewer rupees for each dollar sent from the U.S.

Figures for the end of Ashoj, released last month, had shown the debt above Rs 30 trillion.
