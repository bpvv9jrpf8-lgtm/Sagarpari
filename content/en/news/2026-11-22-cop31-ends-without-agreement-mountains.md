The UN climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye (COP31), ended on Sunday, Nov. 22, without adopting its main decision. The conference was scheduled to run from Nov. 9 to 20, and the hosts added two extra days to try to reach consensus. Even then, countries could not agree on the overarching "cover" decision, called the Antalya Pact.

## Why talks broke down

According to South Korea's Kyunghyang Shinmun, the phrase "phase-out of fossil fuels" was dropped from the second draft. The third draft, released Sunday morning, did not mention coal, oil or fossil fuels at all.

Irish minister Darragh O'Brien, speaking for the European Union, formally objected. The paper reports he said it was unacceptable to conclude a climate agreement with no mention of fossil fuels. Panama also formally objected. According to The Wire, the Marshall Islands refused to agree, citing a lack of climate finance. The African Group complained that the text set no baseline or figure for adaptation and no resource targets for loss and damage.

COP31 President Murat Kurum of Türkiye announced that the decision had not been adopted because parties in the room opposed it. The Wire reports that draft texts on the Mitigation Work Programme and the Global Environment Facility were also not adopted.

## Reactions

"I am deeply disappointed that COP31 has ended without consensus on an overarching outcome," UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said in a statement. UNFCCC Executive Secretary Simon Stiell told the closing ceremony that the world cannot afford a repeat of this moment. India's environment minister, Bhupender Yadav, said India regretted that consensus on the cover decision could not be reached.

Kyunghyang Shinmun notes that this is the first COP since Copenhagen (COP15) in 2009 to run past its deadline and still fail to adopt an official agreement.

## What about Nepal's mountain agenda?

Nepal has pushed for years to give mountains and glaciers a dedicated place in global climate talks. On Sunday afternoon, Onlinekhabar reported that negotiators had agreed at the working level on a two-year "Programme on Mountains and Climate Change." It would focus on adaptation, disaster risk reduction, data collection and early warning systems, with a high-level mountain dialogue planned at COP32.

But Onlinekhabar also noted that the proposal still needed formal approval at the closing plenary. With the main decision not adopted, it was not officially clear at the time of writing whether the mountain provisions will take effect.
