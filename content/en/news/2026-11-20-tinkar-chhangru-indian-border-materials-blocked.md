Development work in Tinkar and Chhangru, two remote villages on Nepal's northern border in Darchula district, has ground to a halt after Indian security forces stopped construction materials from crossing Indian territory. Nepal has no motorable road or usable trail to the villages from its own side, so supplies have to travel along Indian roads to the Sitapul border point. This year, India's Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB) has tightened that route, The Kathmandu Post reported from Tinkar.

## A bridge stuck for two years

A federally funded suspension bridge over the Tinkar stream, linking Tinkar with Langchhupu, is worth around Rs40 million and is roughly 75% complete, according to the report. Work has been stalled for two years because cables and steel plates have not been allowed through; those materials have been sitting in Garbyang, on the Indian side, since November last year.

The contractor, Junction Nirman Sewa, brought 67 workers from Rolpa before Dashain, then had to send them home with no work done. "The company lost Rs1-1.2 million without doing any work," site representative Dhanlal Pun told the Post.

## Chhangru's water crisis

Chhangru, ward 1 of Byas Rural Municipality, has 125 households and has faced a water shortage for about six years. Its two public taps do not supply enough. Residents walk 20 minutes to a well, and the climb back with a load takes 40 to 50 minutes — about an hour for older people.

Villagers pooled their own money, with help from Chhangru natives living abroad, to bring water from a spring at Galendharu about 3.5 km away. In May they bought pipes and fittings in Khalanga and sent them through India to Sitapul, where the SSB stopped them, calling them "construction materials, not household goods." Local resident Hemendra Bohara said goods used to pass with VAT bills and a recommendation letter from the Darchula District Administration Office, but enforcement tightened this year.

Pipes, cement and fittings for a separate federally funded water-lifting project in Chhangru reached the border in June but also could not cross. Monsoon rain ruined the cement, and the rest remains in the open.

## Diplomatic letters haven't helped

According to the contractors, the Indian Embassy in Kathmandu wrote a letter permitting the transit. The report says Nepal's Foreign Ministry also sent a diplomatic note seeking help, and the embassy told Indian customs offices to clear the cargo without delay. Contractors say the SSB ignored the letter.

Darchula Chief District Officer Bartaraj Paudel said contractors have filed a new application that his office is pursuing through the Foreign Ministry, but he would not give a timeline.

Nepal ran a customs office at Sitapul until 2001; it was withdrawn during the Maoist insurgency and never reopened. Darchula customs chief Madhu Khanal said a sub-customs office may follow once the Nepal-India motorable bridge at Khalanga is operating. The Post reports that bridge is scheduled to be inaugurated during Prime Minister Balendra Shah's visit to India.
