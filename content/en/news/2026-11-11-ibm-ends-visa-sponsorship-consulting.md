US technology company IBM says it will end US visa and green card sponsorship for most roles in **IBM Consulting**, its consulting division. According to Reuters, the company shared the news on Tuesday, November 10.

Reuters reports that IBM Consulting employs more than 150,000 people. The company has not said how many employees are directly affected.

## What IBM said

An IBM spokesperson told Reuters that IBM Consulting had used US skilled-worker visa programs to fill skill gaps, but that its use of those programs had been shrinking for years. Because skill availability and client needs have changed, the company decided to stop sponsorship for most of these roles, the spokesperson said.

## Options for affected workers

Bloomberg News, citing an internal company document it reviewed, reported that affected workers can move to IBM roles outside the US or must leave the company by the end of this year. Reuters carried this detail citing Bloomberg. IBM has not yet published which roles are exempt or exactly when the change takes effect.

## Why it matters

Many Nepalis in the US work in technology and consulting on H-1B visas or are in the employment-based green card process. When a large employer stops sponsoring, foreign workers looking for new jobs have fewer options.

The decision comes as the US government tightens skilled-worker visas. According to Reuters, last month the government suspended Microsoft, Adobe and several large outsourcing firms (Cognizant, Infosys, TCS) from the permanent labor certification (PERM) process, the first step toward a green card. Before that, the White House announced a one-time $100,000 fee on new H-1B petitions.

## What to do now

Nepalis at IBM on visa or green card sponsorship should ask the company's HR and immigration team, in writing, how the change applies to them. If you may lose your job, talk to a lawyer early about options for keeping your visa status.
