Jensen Huang, the co-founder of chipmaker Nvidia, has stepped down as chief executive. In a statement released Thursday, November 12, the company said Huang resigned as president, CEO and a member of the board of directors, effective immediately.

Huang helped found Nvidia in 1993 and led it for more than three decades, turning it into the leading maker of the chips that power artificial intelligence (AI).

## Who runs Nvidia now?

Colette Kress, Nvidia's executive vice president and chief financial officer, has been named interim CEO. She will keep her CFO role until a permanent successor is named. The board has started a search and says it will consider both internal and external candidates.

Harvey C. Jones, a former CEO of chip-design software company Synopsys, becomes the first-ever chair of Nvidia's board. In the statement, Jones said Kress helped lead the company through a period of extraordinary growth and has a deep understanding of the business.

Nvidia said it still plans to report results for the third quarter of its fiscal year 2027 on Tuesday, November 17.

## Why did he resign?

According to the Associated Press, the resignation followed reports of extramarital affairs. One was with a woman who, according to The New York Times, U.S. intelligence officials suspect of being a Chinese spy. China denies this. Another was reportedly with an executive who reported to Huang.

Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation into Huang last Sunday, November 8. In an email to employees that day, Huang acknowledged that his actions had hurt his family and wrote that his colleagues deserved better from him. Lawmakers from both parties in Congress are calling for further investigation.

## Impact on shares

The AP reported that Nvidia shares fell sharply during the day on Thursday and closed down more than 4%.

Huang is also a familiar name in Nepal: after the Rasuwa–Bhotekoshi flood in August, he donated $10 million to the Prime Minister's Disaster Relief Fund.

Many Nepali and other immigrant workers in the U.S. own Nvidia indirectly through 401(k) plans, retirement accounts or index funds. Because Nvidia makes up a large share of the S&P 500 and Nasdaq indexes, swings in its stock price affect those funds too.
